Daily evidence brief · Privacy Engineering + AI Governance

Persistent agents need evidence before memory—and proof before trust

Today’s strongest work converges on a control architecture: treat every learned lesson as a testable hypothesis, link memory back to its source trajectory, keep regression probes alive across updates, and separate retrieval isolation from confidentiality. Meanwhile, cryptographic inference proofs and privacy-preserving tree training move assurance closer to executable evidence.

Executive read

Three decisions for this week

  1. Put memory writes behind a promotion gate. Temporary application, falsification tests and archived regression probes are stronger evidence than episode success.
  2. Separate access isolation from confidentiality. A RAG control may block unauthorized retrieval yet still leak embeddings, policy membership or forgeable geometry.
  3. Demand executable assurance. Trace-aware evaluation and bit-exact inference proofs can support audit claims that output-only review cannot.

Governance 1/5Peer-reviewed · KDD 2026

Trace-aware evaluation finds failures that text-only judging misses

Howard Zhang, Chuck Arvin, Logan Brown, Mihir Bhatia, Neelu Choudhary & Lily Miao · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14493

Measured. A three-phase study froze traces from two enterprise analytics agents and compared trace-based LLM judging, text-only judging with red-teaming, and deterministic heuristics. Ninety-seven percent of configurations met the stability threshold. On 72 ground-truth cases, trace-based evaluation reached F1=0.90; text-only evaluation matched recall but produced five times as many false alarms. Functional testing and security red-teaming found 24 novel failures with zero overlap.
Limits / counterevidence. Two analytics agents and frozen traces limit generalization; harness behavior in live, changing workflows may differ. The abstract does not quantify human-review baselines for every failure class.
Why it matters
Final text and functional traces expose different risks; one evaluator cannot substantiate both operational correctness and adversarial resilience.
TPM / engineering application
Pair deterministic trace assertions with adversarial red-team evaluation; version the evaluator, rubric and captured evidence together.
Business outcome hypothesis
A two-framework program may reduce false alarms while surfacing security failures earlier.
Forward implication
Enterprise agent release gates will increasingly require trace evidence, not only answer scoring.

Privacy 1/5Preprint

Private tree training joins DP release protection with TEE processing controls

Moritz Kirschte, Thorsten Peinemann, Kari Kostiainen, Jan Wichelmann, Thomas Eisenbarth & Esfandiar Mohammadi · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14317

Measured / proposed. MammothDP combines a differentially private gradient-boosted tree learner with a privacy filter, trusted execution environment and timing-side-channel hardening. The authors introduce an early-stopping rule that selects the number of trees without additional privacy budget and a rollback-protection design intended to resist timing attacks.
Limits. The abstract reports architecture and security properties but no deployment-scale utility, runtime or independent side-channel evaluation. TEE trust, attestation and rollback assumptions remain part of the security boundary.
Why it matters
Protecting only the released model leaves processing exposure; protecting only processing leaves statistical disclosure at release.
TPM / engineering application
Document one threat model spanning host compromise, side channels, privacy accounting, early stopping and rollback recovery.
Business outcome hypothesis
A combined control could enable sensitive tabular studies with auditable processing and release guarantees.
Forward implication
Health-data platforms may require composable evidence across hardware isolation and formal privacy budgets.

Governance 2/5Preprint

Falsification gates cut harmful persistent updates to 3.6%

Runxuan Tang et al. · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14326

Measured. Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution expresses a proposed lesson as a hypothesis with preconditions, behavior change, expected effect and verifier. It applies the lesson temporarily, then runs target-transfer, falsification and archived-regression probes before commit, refinement or rejection. Across web, app, tool and long-term-memory benchmarks, the authors report 39.8 WebArena success, 78.8 AppWorld average completion, Pass@1 of 0.724/0.487 on τ-Retail/τ-Airline, 5.0% falsification failures, 1.3% regression damage and 3.6% harmful committed updates.
Limits. Preprint results do not establish production reliability, long-duration drift or resistance to compromised verifiers. Residual harmful commits remain non-zero.
Why it matters
Local task success is weak evidence that a behavior should become permanent agent state.
TPM / engineering application
Treat memory changes like code: canary in temporary state, falsify, replay archived tasks, then promote with provenance and rollback.
Business outcome hypothesis
Admission gates may reduce regression incidents while retaining useful adaptation.
Forward implication
Self-improving agents will need change-control records for every persistent lesson.

Privacy 2/5Preprint

RAG policy isolation does not imply embedding confidentiality

Steve Nouyep, Maxime Puys & Sébastien Salva · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14061

Measured. The study audits implementations of geometric access-control schemes across three encoders and four objectives: policy isolation, representation confidentiality, query-forgery resistance and policy-membership hiding. Retrieval utility and policy isolation held, but the other properties did not. A curious user recovered original embeddings from a few hundred aligned pairs; forging policy-compatible queries remained possible at a cost that grew with encoder dimension for secret per-policy transformations.
Limits. Results apply to the tested geometric mechanisms and threat models, not every vector database or access-control architecture. “A few hundred” is the source-supported precision available in the abstract.
Why it matters
An access-control test can pass while protected representations, policy membership or query authorization remain exposed.
TPM / engineering application
Test each security objective independently; add key rotation, aligned-pair limits, membership-leakage tests and server-side authorization.
Business outcome hypothesis
Objective-specific tests may prevent a misleading “encrypted embeddings” assurance claim.
Forward implication
RAG security reviews will distinguish authorization, confidentiality, unforgeability and metadata privacy.

Governance 3/5Preprint · under ACL review

Evidence-linked memory lets agents challenge their own lessons

Yukyeong Lee, Yumin Choi, Jinheon Baek & Sung Ju Hwang · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14320

Measured. WikiTrace stores each lesson with a success/failure explanation and the raw trajectory. At use time, the agent follows those links when an insight lacks detail, conflicts with another or comes from a failed task. Across five agentic benchmarks, the paper reports consistent improvement over existing memory systems and an equal-insight baseline, with lower memory-induced degradation.
Limits. The abstract does not provide effect sizes, model-by-model intervals or operational latency. The work is under review, and evidence retrieval can itself be incomplete or manipulated.
Why it matters
A compact lesson without source evidence hides whether it was inferred correctly or applies to the present task.
TPM / engineering application
Require every reusable lesson to retain trajectory lineage, outcome, timestamp, confidence and a revocation path.
Business outcome hypothesis
Evidence-linked memory may reduce repeated failures and shorten incident reconstruction.
Forward implication
Memory provenance will become a core audit object for long-running agents.

Privacy 3/5Preprint

Federated-learning gradients remain reconstructable under richer subspace constraints

Konstantinos Bouros & Athanasios P. Liavas · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.13976

Measured. The attack exploits the fact that correctly scaled aggregate fully connected-layer gradients form affine, not merely linear, combinations of inputs. Across the paper’s evaluated settings, linear- and affine-subspace constraints substantially improved reconstruction over selected baselines in some cases; moderate batch-size overestimation generally improved or preserved reconstruction even when true batch size was unknown. The authors also tested gradients perturbed by a differential-privacy mechanism.
Limits / counterevidence. Improvements are setting-dependent; the abstract reports no universal success rate or privacy threshold. Results do not prove vulnerability for every architecture, aggregation protocol or DP configuration.
Why it matters
Uncertain batch size is not a reliable defense, and aggregate gradients can encode exploitable geometric structure.
TPM / engineering application
Red-team client updates using affine-subspace attacks before selecting clipping, noise, secure aggregation and minimum cohort size.
Business outcome hypothesis
Attack-informed parameter selection may reduce reconstruction exposure without blindly overspending utility.
Forward implication
Federated-learning privacy claims will need attack suites tied to the actual layer and aggregation design.

Governance 4/5Preprint · open code/runs

Persistent agent harnesses fail a simple continual-learning floor

Daniel Lenton · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14505

Measured. Continual-ARC streams tasks from all three ARC-AGI editions, repeats them after dormant gaps with new instances, charges for demonstrations and retries, then freezes the learner for memorization and generalization tests. Experiments on Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash found that harness persistence generally did not improve memorization without explicit expert guidance. Most harness methods learned less than a floor that simply cached task demonstrations; none improved on unseen tasks, while weight-based learning did.
Limits. One task family and two primary backbones do not cover enterprise workflows. The benchmark measures acquisition under its feedback-cost model, not deployed business value.
Why it matters
Long-lived memory can create the appearance of learning without durable skill acquisition or transfer.
TPM / engineering application
Evaluate reacquisition cost, dormant-gap retention and unseen-task transfer against a cache-only floor.
Business outcome hypothesis
A stronger learning benchmark could prevent spending on persistence that only replays demonstrations.
Forward implication
Agent “learning” claims will need separate memorization, transfer and cost-adjusted retention evidence.

Privacy 4/5Preprint / architecture

Signed semantic memory pairs private retrieval with local inference

Vicenç Torra & Maria Bras-Amorós · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.13696

Proposed / implemented. The architecture signs knowledge-base content to deter semantic-memory poisoning, performs Prolog-like Horn-clause inference locally, and accesses external knowledge privately. The authors provide Python implementations at multiple privacy levels, including k-anonymity and information-theoretic private information retrieval.
Limits. The abstract reports an architecture and implementations, not comparative attack success, end-to-end latency or production-scale evaluation. Signatures protect integrity and provenance, not truth, freshness or confidentiality by themselves.
Why it matters
Agent memory needs separate controls for who may query it, whether content was altered, and whether the content is actually valid.
TPM / engineering application
Sign knowledge objects, verify before inference, isolate local reasoning, and log privacy mode plus key/version provenance.
Business outcome hypothesis
Signed private retrieval could reduce poisoning and query exposure in cross-organization agents.
Forward implication
Agent-memory platforms may combine cryptographic lineage with private retrieval rather than rely on prompt-level trust.

Governance 5/5Theoretical preprint

Reward can improve while optimal-response coverage collapses

Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy & Dylan J. Foster · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14467

Proved. The analysis shows REINFORCE-style optimization can drive optimal-response probability near zero even while reward increases; convergence may then require exponentially many steps despite good initial coverage, and base-model KL regularization alone does not prevent the failure. Repeatedly resetting the KL reference to the latest policy is formalized through Approximate Policy Mirror Descent, with global convergence guarantees for stylized PPO/GRPO and squared-loss variants under softmax linear policies over pretrained features.
Limits. These are theory results under specified policy classes and approximations, not direct evidence that a production model will improve with reference resets. Empirical safety and capability effects still need evaluation.
Why it matters
Average reward can hide the disappearance of rare but valuable behaviors from the policy’s reachable support.
TPM / engineering application
Track response coverage and rare-capability probes across post-training, not reward alone; test reset schedules as controlled experiments.
Business outcome hypothesis
Coverage monitoring may reduce costly regressions that top-line reward metrics conceal.
Forward implication
Model-change governance will include support/coverage evidence alongside reward and safety scores.

Privacy 5/5Preprint · open code

Bit-exact inference proofs cost 41–50× serving time

Tomer Solberg · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14083

Measured. Sable proves that a committed integer-quantized transformer produced a specific token sequence from a prompt. On one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU, it proves 64-layer Qwen2.5-32B inference in 92.5 seconds for 4,096 tokens and 398 seconds for 16,384; Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-30B-A3B take 121.8 and 84.7 seconds for 4,096 tokens. Proof cost is reported at 41–50× serving time or 6.4 cents per 4,096-token request; a one-core verifier takes 19–56 seconds. The soundness error is 2^-103 in the random-oracle model.
Limits / counterevidence. The proof is not zero-knowledge and targets open-weight models. Integer execution matches BF16 greedy tokens at 86.3% of WikiText-2 positions and is within 1.6 MMLU and 1.7 GSM8K points on Qwen3—not bit-identical to ordinary BF16 serving.
Why it matters
It converts “this committed model produced this output” from a log assertion into a verifiable computation claim.
TPM / engineering application
Pilot proofs for a small sample of regulated, disputed or high-value inferences; retain prompt/output commitments and verifier logs.
Business outcome hypothesis
Selective proofs may improve auditability where full-time proving is too costly.
Forward implication
Cryptographic inference receipts may become a tiered assurance option for open-weight deployments.

Research to Build

Two-week experiment

Memory-change promotion gate with retention and privacy regressions

Combine FGSE’s temporary hypothesis testing, WikiTrace’s evidence links and Continual-ARC’s dormant-gap retention design into a control that decides whether a learned lesson may enter persistent agent state.

Protocol

  1. Freeze a baseline agent, 100 representative tasks, 25 dormant-gap repeats, 25 unseen tasks and a privacy/security attack set.
  2. Represent every proposed memory lesson as preconditions, behavior change, expected effect, verifier and source-trajectory links.
  3. Apply the lesson only to an isolated candidate state; block external side effects during evaluation.
  4. Measure target gain, transfer, falsification failures, archived regressions, privacy-policy violations and inference cost.
  5. Repeat retained tasks after a seven-day gap with no fresh demonstrations; compare against no-memory and cache-only floors.
  6. Promote only with signed evidence, scoped applicability, expiry, owner and one-click rollback; otherwise refine or reject.

Predeclared evidence

  • Target delta versus frozen baseline.
  • Regression damage on archived tasks.
  • Falsification rate on counterexamples.
  • Dormant-gap retention and unseen-task transfer.
  • Privacy/security violation rate under attack probes.
  • Evidence coverage: share of lessons linked to source traces and valid verifiers.

Promotion rule hypothesis: require positive target gain, no critical privacy/security regression, a predeclared upper bound on harmful regressions, and performance above the cache-only floor. These thresholds must be set locally; the papers do not validate a universal gate.

Three near-term signals to watch

01

Independent memory-gate replication

Whether FGSE’s low harmful-commit and regression rates hold with longer deployments, different verifiers and adversarially poisoned evidence.

02

RAG confidentiality fixes

Whether geometric access-control systems add tested protection against embedding recovery, query forgery and policy-membership leakage—not only retrieval isolation.

03

Proof economics

Whether bit-exact inference proofs gain zero-knowledge, lower prover overhead and production attestation integrations without widening the BF16 behavior gap.

One practical action

Block one persistent memory write path behind an evidence gate

Choose the agent with the highest-impact retained memory. For every proposed lesson, require source-trajectory links, a temporary canary, falsification and archived-regression tests, privacy/security probes, expiry and rollback. Publish the first promote/refine/reject ledger to your governance review.
