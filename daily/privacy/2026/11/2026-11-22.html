Today’s thesis: model assurance is becoming execution assurance. A model can hide memorized data, a federated-learning participant can silently skip required controls, and a hosted model can change under the same name. Governance therefore needs evidence of code, dataflow, runtime configuration and change—not just a model card.

Executive readout

Two conference systems sharpen this week’s direction: GradLock shows how compromised training components can store recoverable private examples inside model parameters, while ExclaveFL shows how an attested dataflow graph can prove that required transformations actually ran. The practical synthesis is to test for leakage and prove execution integrity together.

Privacy & security · 01

Compromised training components can turn weights into a covert data vault

Jin-Seong Kim et al. · 30 Jul 2026; presented at ACM CCS ’26, 15–19 Nov 2026 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2607.27886

Measured finding
GradLock injected sensitive samples during training and recovered data from final models with reported SSIM near 1.0 and extraction under one second across MNIST, Imagenette and CelebA. A deployment study reported that 93.3% of participants did not detect the malicious logic.
Method & limitations
Stateless deterministic indexing allocates an isolated parameter “vault,” while gradient locking protects the payload through optimization. The study uses selected vision datasets and attack-controlled components; it does not establish prevalence in real software supply chains.
Why it matters
Post-training privacy tests can miss malicious behavior introduced by a dependency, trainer or pre-processing component.
TPM application
Require signed training images, dependency provenance, reproducible builds and post-training canary-extraction tests.
Business outcome hypothesis
Joint provenance and extraction testing may reduce the chance that a compliant training job ships a compromised artifact.
Forward implication
AI software-bill-of-materials programs will expand from inventory to behavioral attestation.
Privacy & security · 02

Federated-learning claims can be backed by attested dataflow evidence

Jinnan Guo, Kapil Vaswani, Andrew Paverd & Peter Pietzuch · revised 14 Nov 2026; ACM SoCC ’26 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2412.10537 · DOI

Measured finding
ExclaveFL builds runtime-attested dataflow graphs covering client transformations and server aggregation. Its AMD SEV-SNP-compatible variant introduced less than 12% overhead versus unprotected training.
Method & limitations
Integrity-only “exclaves” produce proofs without relying on confidential enclave memory. Native exclaves require proprietary firmware changes; the evaluated compatible variant provides slightly weaker guarantees and does not by itself prove source-data truthfulness.
Why it matters
An auditor can verify that sanitization, aggregation or other required steps ran instead of trusting participant declarations.
TPM application
Prototype attested evidence for privacy-transform and aggregation gates in cross-organization FL.
Business outcome hypothesis
Cryptographic execution evidence may lower audit friction and dispute costs for federated programs.
Forward implication
Model documentation will increasingly link to machine-verifiable lineage graphs.
Privacy & security · 03

Federated averaging does not prevent exact recovery of client text

Omkar Shrikanth et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24689

Measured finding
An iterative gradient-inversion attack reported 100% precision and exact recovery of up to 80.2% of client sentences when aggregates contained up to four times more tokens than the model hidden dimension; existing attacks recovered none exactly in that regime.
Method & limitations
The attack subtracts gradients for exactly recovered sentences and repeats on the residual. Results are configuration-specific and do not establish success against secure aggregation, clipping or calibrated differential privacy.
Why it matters
Federated architecture alone does not constitute a confidentiality control for language training.
TPM application
Add empirical reconstruction red-teaming and require quantified privacy mechanisms before approving federated text workloads.
Business outcome hypothesis
Attack-based validation can prevent overstated privacy claims and costly redesign after launch.
Forward implication
Federated-learning assurance will include recoverability tests, not only data-location diagrams.
Privacy & security · 04

Local differential privacy needs integrity and dropout controls

Haochen Sun, Jacob Imola & Xi He · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24623

Measured finding
A three-message proof-of-plaintext-evaluation framework restricted manipulation to selective dropout, with mean per-client online latency below 500 ms for evaluated PI-RAPPOR and Hadamard Response settings. Theory gives a square-root-of-domain-size reduction in attack-dependent error versus unrestricted manipulation.
Method & limitations
The work covers histogram estimation and specific mechanisms; selective dropout remains possible and production device/network costs are not established.
Why it matters
Formal local privacy does not ensure that reported telemetry is honest.
TPM application
Pair privacy-budget reviews with poisoning, replay, report-validity and dropout threat tests.
Business outcome hypothesis
Lightweight verification may improve telemetry trust without full homomorphic-computation cost.
Forward implication
Privacy-preserving telemetry will carry joint confidentiality-and-integrity evidence.
Privacy & security · 05

Clean final answers conceal widespread intermediate privacy violations

Ivoline C. Ngong et al. · 5 Mar 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2603.04902

Measured finding
AgentSCOPE evaluated seven models on 62 multi-tool scenarios across eight regulatory domains. Final-output leakage ranged from 24–40%, while violations anywhere in the pipeline reached 82–94%.
Method & limitations
A Contextual Integrity Privacy Flow Graph labels instruction, query, tool-response and output boundaries. The benchmark centers on one fictional persona and 62 scenarios; online and enterprise generalization remain open.
Why it matters
Most violations arise before the final answer, especially when tools over-return sensitive fields.
TPM application
Define necessary fields per action and enforce schema-level response filtering before model exposure.
Business outcome hypothesis
Boundary minimization may reduce hidden exposure without depending on the model to suppress seen data.
Forward implication
Agent privacy audits will follow information flows, not only conversations.
Governance & reliability · 06

User-simulator benchmarks must prove they detect bad simulated behavior

Hengguang Zhou et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24892

Measured finding
Metrics from three prior studies detected only 5–6 of ten deliberately seeded simulator failures; combined scores favored populations instructed to accept broken work. A new suite detected all ten.
Method & limitations
Six simulators were compared with 351 real sessions from 348 users across 23 languages. Seeded-failure detection does not prove ecological validity for every domain.
Why it matters
A high synthetic-user score can reward an agent by weakening the user’s actual goal.
TPM application
Fault-inject changed goals, omitted constraints, premature stops and broken-work acceptance before using simulators for release gates.
Business outcome hypothesis
Meta-evaluation may reduce false launch confidence and wasted human validation.
Forward implication
Benchmark documentation will list failures that its evaluator can and cannot detect.
Governance & reliability · 07

Self-evolving agents can improve overall while forgetting earlier work

Xiaoyin Chen et al. · 19 Oct 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2610.25858

Measured finding
Across four drifting streams built from BIRD, BFCL and ScienceWorld, ReasoningBank, GEPA and Meta-Harness improved aggregate performance on most streams, but greater plasticity consistently reduced stability; retrieval showed foreign-memory and workflow-collision errors.
Method & limitations
Three frozen-weight self-evolution approaches cover experience retrieval and self-modification. Results do not define a universal frontier for proprietary or production agents.
Why it matters
Average gains can mask regressions on older and unseen workflows.
TPM application
Gate every memory, prompt or skill mutation on old-task retention, current-task gain and unseen-task transfer.
Business outcome hypothesis
Regression-aware change control may reduce silent workflow damage.
Forward implication
Self-evolution will be managed like software deployment—with diffs, canaries and rollback.
Governance & reliability · 08

Fairness results depend on the numerical execution contract

Erzheng Zhang & Agnes Chu · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint, under review · arXiv 2611.23657

Measured finding
Holding model, records, threshold and audit code fixed, a 24B model served with vLLM FP8 selected 54% of people versus 45% under strict FP32 and changed a diagnostic sex-audit verdict.
Method & limitations
Three tabular families and two open-weight LLMs were tested across arithmetic and deployment precision. Every changed verdict had a sampling interval containing the criterion; the study does not show FP8 is fairer.
Why it matters
Model-file identity is insufficient for reproducible assurance.
TPM application
Version precision, hardware, kernels, engine, thresholds, tests and uncertainty with every audit.
Business outcome hypothesis
Configuration-stability tests may reduce contradictory compliance conclusions.
Forward implication
Audit evidence will bind to the serving stack, not just the weights.
Governance & reliability · 09

Hosted-model audit evidence expires when providers change the target

Mika Facchini, Technical University of Munich · 16 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.20901

Measured finding
Across 248,400 API calls, five providers and three audits over 13 months, the same identifier showed a significantly different bias pattern one year later; successors retained bias in changed size and form. Provider notice periods ranged from zero to six months.
Method & limitations
Venture-screening profiles varied gender, origin and university. Results are domain-specific and observational.
Why it matters
A passed audit can become stale with no application-code change.
TPM application
Attach validity windows, response-level version records and automatic re-audit triggers to provider/model changes.
Business outcome hypothesis
Expiry-aware evidence can reduce false compliance confidence.
Forward implication
One-time model certificates will give way to continuously versioned evidence.
Governance & reliability · 10

Agent memory must be governed as persistent user information

Chirag Goel et al. · 15 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.20144

Measured finding
HEAMS compares memory systems across performance, efficiency, security, privacy and user sovereignty. No system leads every performance benchmark; strong utility can coexist with poor privacy and sovereignty. Recall restrictions reduced inappropriate disclosure at a helpfulness cost, while recall and retention policies improved poisoning resistance.
Method & limitations
The work combines an open benchmark suite, prototype memory system and governance wrapper. It acknowledges coverage gaps and does not provide one universal effect size.
Why it matters
Persistent memories can resist correction, outlive user intent or influence later actions after poisoning.
TPM application
Add deletion, expiry, provenance, disclosure, poisoning and correction tests to memory acceptance criteria.
Business outcome hypothesis
Memory-specific assurance may improve user trust while imposing a measurable helpfulness tradeoff.
Forward implication
Memory components will receive assurance cases separate from the foundation model.

Top impactful research and applications

GradLock — ACM CCS ’26 conference paper

Evidence quality: three image datasets, deployment-detection study and optimization-resilience tests. Limit: controlled attack and selected modalities. Application hypothesis: signed build provenance plus secret canary extraction tests can expose compromised training components before release. Expected impact: stronger supply-chain assurance; effectiveness must be validated against the organization’s actual stack.

ExclaveFL — ACM SoCC ’26 conference paper

Evidence quality: implemented system, runtime proofs and measured overhead. Limit: proprietary-firmware dependency and incomplete input truthfulness. Application hypothesis: attach attested dataflow evidence to privacy-transform and aggregation controls. Expected impact: more reproducible audits and fewer trust-by-declaration dependencies.

User-simulator meta-evaluation — preprint, 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: ten injected failures and 351 real multilingual sessions. Limit: seeded faults are not comprehensive realism. Application hypothesis: make evaluator fault-detection a prerequisite for synthetic-user release evidence. Expected impact: fewer misleading benchmark passes.

Self-evolving agents as continual learners — preprint, 19 Oct 2026

Evidence quality: three methods across four drifting streams. Limit: benchmark and method scope. Application hypothesis: mutation-level retention and transfer gates can make self-evolution a controlled deployment process. Expected impact: reduced regression risk, not guaranteed operational improvement.

HEAMS — preprint, 15 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: open multi-dimensional benchmark and governance wrapper. Limit: acknowledged scenario gaps. Application hypothesis: a memory acceptance scorecard can make deletion, sovereignty and poisoning resistance auditable. Expected impact: more transparent privacy–helpfulness tradeoffs.

Research to Build: Attested Privacy Canary Pipeline

Goal: test whether a training pipeline can both detect hidden memorization and prove that required controls executed.

Prototype

  1. Create 24 synthetic canary records split across text and image training shards; never use real personal data.
  2. Run three builds: approved baseline, compromised-component simulation and defense-enabled pipeline.
  3. Record signed artifact hashes, dependency identities, transformation steps, privacy configuration and aggregation events in an append-only evidence graph.
  4. After training, run extraction attempts, membership inference and weight-difference scans; attempt to omit one required transformation to test attestation failure.

Metrics and release gate

  • Canary exact-recovery and similarity rate; membership-attack advantage.
  • Attestation coverage and false acceptance of omitted or modified steps.
  • Task utility, training overhead and evidence-verification time.
  • Gate: no critical canary recovery, no unverified transform, complete artifact-to-evidence linkage, and a reproducible rollback path.

Evidence boundary This is a proposed enterprise experiment inspired by GradLock and ExclaveFL. Neither paper establishes the effectiveness of this combined control; local validation and independent security review are required.

Three near-term signals to watch

1 · Runtime-attested lineageWhether model registries begin accepting machine-verifiable training and transformation graphs as first-class evidence.
2 · Supply-chain privacy red teamsWhether dependency scanners add behavioral leakage tests instead of stopping at package provenance and known vulnerabilities.
3 · Memory-specific assuranceWhether platforms expose retention, deletion, correction and poisoning-resistance evidence for agent memory components.
