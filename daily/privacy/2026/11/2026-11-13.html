Daily evidence brief · Privacy Engineering + AI Governance

Govern the state you cannot see—not just the answer you can

Today’s evidence moves assurance below the response layer: recurrent memory can retain private facts, co-resident workloads can infer approval policy, and persistent agent learning still struggles to beat simple caching. The governance unit is the full state lifecycle—creation, reuse, isolation, verification, expiry and attribution.

Executive read

Three control decisions

  1. Treat retained state as sensitive data. Isolation, access, telemetry and deletion controls must cover caches and recurrent representations, not only prompts and outputs.
  2. Put approval enforcement outside the model. A confidential policy in a prompt can leak through execution behavior and should not be the authority boundary.
  3. Make learning earn promotion. Persistent memory should outperform a cache, retain skills after dormant gaps and avoid privacy regression before production use.

10 high-signal developments

Five privacy/security and five governance/reliability developments selected for state-lifecycle, assurance and enterprise-adoption relevance.

Privacy 1/5Preprint

Recurrent inference state can preserve private facts beyond the prompt

Weimin Fu, Shikun Liu, Qiying Li, Yuntao Liu, Pan Li, Xiaolong Guo · 11 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.16048

Measured. The study separates recoverability, persistence through public dialogue, causal influence and power leakage in released hybrid recurrent/attention models. Partial-state probes predicted private facts and response rules; personal-attribute signals generalized to unseen authors and persisted after public dialogue. Under a fixed schedule, complete-read power traces recovered private facts; on a partitioned GPU, an unprivileged account inferred age from whole-device power during public requests.
Limits. The abstract does not report universal attack rates. Results concern released hybrid implementations and specific observation capabilities; they do not establish leakage for every recurrent model or accelerator.
Why it matters
Deleting a prompt or hiding a response does not erase private information retained in model-serving state or its physical execution.
TPM / engineering application
Add recurrent-state lifetime, tenant isolation, zeroization, telemetry access and side-channel testing to the privacy threat model.
Business outcome hypothesis
State-aware isolation may reduce cross-tenant exposure and strengthen deletion evidence.
Forward implication
Confidential inference claims will need to cover transient and recurrent state plus accelerator observability.

Governance 1/5Preprint · benchmark

Persistent agent memory does not automatically produce learning

Daniel Lenton · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.14505

Measured. Continual-ARC streams recurring and novel ARC tasks over a simulated working lifetime, charges a feedback budget, freezes the learner, then tests fresh instances of seen and unseen tasks. Across Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash harnesses, persistence generally failed to improve memorization without explicit expert guidance; most harness methods learned less than a floor that cached demonstrations. No harness method improved unseen-task performance, while the evaluated weight-trained learner did.
Limits. ARC isolates abstract skill acquisition and does not model enterprise tools, privacy policies or real user drift. The abstract provides qualitative cross-method findings rather than a single effect size.
Why it matters
Accumulating experiences can look like continual learning while delivering no transfer beyond retrieval.
TPM / engineering application
Benchmark persistent agents against a cache-only floor, dormant-gap retention and unseen-task transfer before calling them self-improving.
Business outcome hypothesis
A stronger promotion gate may prevent storage cost and risk that produce no operational learning.
Forward implication
Enterprise agent evaluations will separate recall, skill retention, transfer and feedback efficiency.

Privacy 2/5Preprint · side channel

A neighboring GPU process inferred an agent’s approval policy

Weimin Fu et al. · 12 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.16158

Measured. PolicySniff profiles two candidate approval settings, then times only the attacker’s own kernels while the agent handles an identical task suffix with the policy prefix already cached. On an eager sparse mixture-of-experts runtime, a frozen blinded study identified the deployed policy at 94.17% balanced accuracy. Accuracy was 88.82% when both policies produced the same decision and 91.98% when decisions differed.
Limits. The attacker knows candidate policies and tasks and profiles the same runtime. Results do not establish transfer to dense models, other schedulers, hardware or isolation controls.
Why it matters
Behavioral compliance can coexist with configuration leakage; the secret is encoded in execution even when prompts and responses are hidden.
TPM / engineering application
Test policy distinguishability under co-tenancy, partition sensitive workloads and enforce approvals in a deterministic external service.
Business outcome hypothesis
Runtime isolation may reduce policy reconnaissance and strengthen multi-tenant trust.
Forward implication
Agent security reviews will treat policy confidentiality and policy enforcement as distinct requirements.

Governance 2/5Preprint · NIST evaluation

Human preference and automated RAG coverage select different systems

Oleg Zendel, Kun Ran, Chenglong Ma, Nguyen Khoi Vo, Shuai Wang · 12 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.16425

Measured. RMIT-ADM+S submitted seven research-agent runs to the TREC 2026 RAG track. Six exceeded the track median across all three rubric/nugget collections; the best submission tied the Batch-1 maximum of 0.892 for vital coverage. Yet human pairwise preference changed by batch: an open-weight run led Batch 1 despite lower automated coverage, while a different research-first run led Batch 2.
Limits. Seven submissions on one TREC corpus do not identify a universal best design. Human preference, rubric coverage and factual correctness are different constructs.
Why it matters
A single automated score can optimize coverage while missing the qualities users value.
TPM / engineering application
Pair rubric coverage with blinded human preference, source correctness and cost; require batch-level variance, not only an average.
Business outcome hypothesis
Multi-metric selection may reduce evaluator gaming and improve customer acceptance.
Forward implication
Research-agent procurement will require construct-valid evidence rather than one leaderboard rank.

Privacy 3/5Peer-reviewed · IEEE Access

Multi-agent privacy exposure is dominated by internal channels

Faouzi El Yagoubi, Godwin Badu-Marfo, Ranwa Al Mallah · 17 Jun 2026 · IEEE Access 14

Measured. AgentLeak evaluates 1,000 scenarios spanning healthcare, finance, legal and corporate domains over 4,979 traces from five models. The published study reports final-output leakage of 27.2% in multi-agent configurations versus 43.2% in single-agent systems, but OR-aggregated exposure across output, inter-agent and shared channels reached 68.9%; inter-agent messages leaked at 68.8%.
Limits / counterevidence. Aggregate exposure depends on the benchmark’s scenario mix, leakage taxonomy and OR composition. Lower final-output leakage is real within the study but does not compensate for added internal surfaces.
Why it matters
An output-only privacy review can approve a system whose coordination layer is the primary disclosure path.
TPM / engineering application
Instrument and policy-test messages, tool arguments, shared memory and logs; minimize sensitive fields before agent handoff.
Business outcome hypothesis
Internal-channel controls may reduce incident scope and improve evidence for audits.
Forward implication
Multi-agent platforms will expose privacy telemetry and enforcement at every coordination boundary.

Governance 3/5Preprint · dynamic benchmark

Static agent success does not predict reliability in a changing world

Pengcheng Zhou et al. · 12 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.17267

Measured. LiveGeo places agents in GeoWorld, where geographic states evolve while verifiable state histories are preserved. Agents must reacquire evidence, test whether prior support remains valid and revise plans. The authors report that no frontier model consistently excelled across static tasks and that strong static results did not ensure dynamic reliability; the main bottleneck was acquiring relevant evidence, translating it into valid decisions and preserving correctness as the world changed.
Limits. The abstract does not provide a single quantitative reliability gap. Geographic tasks may not transfer directly to enterprise workflow drift.
Why it matters
Many production failures come from stale evidence, not inability to solve the initial task.
TPM / engineering application
Attach freshness, provenance and revalidation triggers to decision evidence; replay tasks after controlled state changes.
Business outcome hypothesis
Freshness-aware agents may reduce stale-action incidents and improve operational resilience.
Forward implication
Agent SLAs will include evidence shelf life and recovery after environmental change.

Privacy 4/5Preprint · open implementations

Signed semantic memory can combine integrity with private retrieval

Vicenç Torra, Maria Bras-Amorós · 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.13696

Proposed and implemented. The paper separates a local Prolog-like inference engine from an external signed knowledge base, then implements private retrieval with multiple privacy levels including k-anonymity and information-theoretic private information retrieval. Signatures are intended to detect unauthorized knowledge modification while private access hides which knowledge is retrieved.
Limits. This is an architectural proposal with prototype implementations; the abstract does not report end-to-end attack resistance, overhead or enterprise-scale evaluation. Signatures establish origin/integrity, not truth or authorization.
Why it matters
Memory integrity and query privacy solve different problems and must be composed deliberately.
TPM / engineering application
Version and sign memory entries, verify before use, separate retrieval privacy from access control and log proof outcomes.
Business outcome hypothesis
Composed controls may reduce poisoning risk without revealing a user’s knowledge-access pattern.
Forward implication
Agent memory services will expose provenance and private-query guarantees as separate product properties.

Governance 4/5Preprint · research infrastructure

Architecture claims need a record of failed ideas and scaling assumptions

Jie Wang et al. · 12 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.17332

Proposed infrastructure. ArchSpace records each architecture proposal with experiments and reasoning, supports community review and links later work back to prior evidence. It supplies Qwen3.5 and OLMo3 baselines, fitted scaling laws and a reusable training recipe so small-scale experiments can estimate larger-scale loss before costly training.
Limits. Scaling-law extrapolation is model- and regime-dependent, and community review does not guarantee correctness. The paper proposes evidence infrastructure; it does not establish that reviewed ideas succeed at production scale.
Why it matters
Selective publication hides abandoned approaches, preventing teams from understanding the denominator behind architecture claims.
TPM / engineering application
Require hypothesis, baseline, compute budget, negative result, scaling assumption and decision rationale in an architecture evidence register.
Business outcome hypothesis
A reusable evidence trail may reduce duplicated experiments and improve capital-allocation decisions.
Forward implication
Model governance will track failed and superseded experiments alongside released checkpoints.

Privacy 5/5Preprint · open benchmark

Pixel-space steering suppresses soft-biometric disclosure across VLMs

Ruofan Xing, Arman Akbari, Weikai Lin, Yuhao Zhu, Xuan Zhang · revised 10 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.10528

Measured. PrivMark learns one bounded image perturbation from two leakage-related activation directions: incidental demographic description and compliance with direct biometric probes. BioleakBench contains 1,600 VQA pairs. Across three white-box source models, the paper reports up to 85.9% biometric suppression while retaining 75% of clean OK-VQA utility and up to 1.4× the privacy–utility trade-off of the strongest pixel-only baseline; perturbations also transferred to seven unseen VLMs, including closed APIs.
Limits. “Up to” results are not worst-case guarantees. Adaptive attackers, image transformations, new models and legitimate accessibility or clinical uses may change both privacy and utility.
Why it matters
Soft biometrics can be inferred from distributed image cues even when conventional face de-identification is applied.
TPM / engineering application
Evaluate demographic suppression and task utility after the real resize/compression pipeline, including direct probes and open-ended descriptions.
Business outcome hypothesis
Optional image-side protection may reduce unintended demographic disclosure without requiring API-provider changes.
Forward implication
Media privacy tooling will distinguish identity hiding from attribute-disclosure suppression.

Governance 5/5Preprint · HITL framework

A valid proof artefact may still verify the wrong claim

György Eigner, Patrik P. Süli, Roland Hollós · 9 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.13042

Demonstrated framework. The authors separate tool validation, artefact checking, fidelity auditing, fixed human decision points and attribution. They implement tool layers for mathematics and statistics and run the mathematical workflow on a two-claim example: an ambiguous claim was split into two readings, one refuted and one proved. The paper notes that a machine-checked translation can differ semantically from the author’s text.
Limits. One small demonstration does not establish operational effectiveness or reviewer capacity at research scale. Faithfulness can be undecidable in general, so the framework manages rather than eliminates semantic risk.
Why it matters
Passing a verifier proves an artefact property, not that the artefact faithfully represents the intended business, scientific or policy claim.
TPM / engineering application
Bind every automated check to a normalized claim, evidence scope, tool version, human decision and human/AI contribution record.
Business outcome hypothesis
Claim-to-check traceability may reduce false assurance and improve accountability for AI-generated analyses.
Forward implication
High-stakes agent outputs will require fidelity evidence in addition to execution logs and verifier success.

Research to Build

Two-week assurance prototype

State-lifetime and learning-retention promotion gate

Combine the recurrent-state privacy threat model with Continual-ARC’s cache floor: test whether a persistent agent learns useful skills while ensuring its retained state does not expose private facts or approval policy.

Protocol

  1. Select 12 recurring workflow skills with synthetic private facts, two approval policies and four dormant-gap lengths.
  2. Run three arms: no persistence, cache-only, and the proposed persistent-memory learner; freeze model and harness versions.
  3. After each gap, measure fresh-instance success, feedback consumed, unseen-task transfer and regression on earlier skills.
  4. Probe outputs, inter-agent messages, memory reads and retained state for synthetic facts; test policy distinguishability under permitted co-tenancy telemetry.
  5. Rotate or delete the private facts and verify behavioral removal plus state zeroization evidence.
  6. Promote only if the learner beats the cache floor, retains earlier skills, adds no critical privacy leakage and stays within cost/latency limits.

Evidence package

  • Learning lift over cache-only.
  • Dormant-gap retention by skill.
  • Unseen-task transfer after freeze.
  • Privacy leakage by state surface.
  • Policy distinguishability under co-tenancy.
  • Deletion verification before and after zeroization.

Decision-rule hypothesis: persistence is justified only when the lower confidence bound on task benefit exceeds the cache floor and no critical private fact or approval-policy signal crosses the local release threshold. The cited studies do not supply universal thresholds.

Three near-term signals to watch

01

State-isolation replication

Whether recurrent-state and approval-policy attacks reproduce across dense models, newer GPU schedulers, confidential-computing boundaries and realistic multi-tenant noise.

02

Learning beyond caching

Whether persistent agents can reliably exceed a demonstration cache on dormant retention and unseen-task transfer without increasing privacy or security regressions.

03

Evidence shelf-life SLAs

Whether dynamic-agent platforms expose freshness metadata, revalidation triggers and measured recovery after the external world changes.

One practical action

Add a “retained state” row to one agent privacy review

For prompt cache, recurrent state, memory store and coordination log, record: owner, tenant boundary, private content class, access path, retention/zeroization rule, telemetry exposure, policy dependency and test evidence. A blank row is a visible control gap, not “no data.”
