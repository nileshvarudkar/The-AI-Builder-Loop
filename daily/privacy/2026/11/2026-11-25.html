Agent memory is becoming a regulated data system.

Recent audits show that what an agent displays, stores, recalls, and deletes can diverge. The engineering response is a memory control plane: admission, lineage, recall policy, withdrawal propagation, and replayable evidence.

Executive read

Decision thesis. Persistent memory should be governed as derived personal data, not treated as a convenience feature. FORGETMENOT’s controlled audit of roughly 3,800 sessions across five commercial agents found that user-visible memory exposed only about 20% of attributes the agents appeared to store, while every tested agent showed non-zero post-deletion leakage. Separately, HEAMS found that strong performance can coexist with weak privacy, security, or user sovereignty.

Operational implication: “view” and “delete” controls need independent verification across chat history, extracted memory, embeddings, caches, derived preferences, skills, and backups. A successful UI action is not evidence that downstream influence is gone.

Evidence convention: “Measured” is limited to the cited evaluation. “Formal result” states the authors’ assumptions. “Interpretation,” “application,” and “forecast” are explicitly labeled and are not claims of proven operational impact.

10 high-signal developments

Privacy engineeringPreprint

1. FORGETMENOT finds a memory transparency and deletion gap

Ismat Jarin, Tu Le & Athina Markopoulou · 22 November 2026 · arXiv 2611.29804

Measured. The audit generated roughly 3,800 multi-turn sessions and about 6 million tokens across five commercial AI agents. User-visible memory revealed about 20% of the attributes the agents appeared to retain. A stealth-memory-injection technique increased stored personal/sensitive attributes by 25% for Copilot and 15% for Claude; all five systems showed non-zero post-deletion leakage. A proof-of-concept third-party email test measured 31.9% leakage for Gemini.

Limits. Results are version-, prompt-, account-, and product-policy-dependent. Behavioral inference cannot expose every internal storage mechanism, and vendor implementations may change.

Why it matters. A visible memory list may not be a complete access or deletion ledger.

TPM application (proposed). Add black-box write–inspect–delete–probe tests with seeded health, demographic, and contact attributes to release and quarterly controls.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Independent memory assurance may reduce deletion and trust risk, but it will require durable test accounts, version capture, and escalation paths for silent regressions.

Privacy / securityPreprint · open-source benchmark

2. HEAMS evaluates memory as a custodian, not just a retrieval layer

Chirag Goel et al. · 15 November 2026 · arXiv 2611.20144 · Code

Measured. HEAMS defines behavioral tests spanning performance, efficiency, security, privacy, and user sovereignty; it evaluates multiple memory systems and a prototype governance wrapper. No memory system leads every performance benchmark. Recall restrictions reduce inappropriate disclosure but trade off helpfulness, while recall and retention policies improve poisoning resistance.

Limits. The authors acknowledge benchmark gaps. Results depend on selected systems, tasks, judges, policies, and attack cases; enterprise effectiveness is untested.

Why it matters. Retrieval accuracy alone can reward a memory that is useful but unsafe or hard to control.

TPM application (proposed). Add privacy, deletion, poisoning, and user-control columns to the memory-system scorecard and prevent a single aggregate score from masking failures.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Multi-objective evaluation may avoid unsafe vendor selection, while exposing real product trade-offs that need risk-owner decisions rather than metric averaging.

Privacy architecturePreprint · thematic review

3. Self-evolving personal agents need withdrawal across every state carrier

Zibin Lin, Taotao Wang & Shengli Zhang · 20 November 2026 · arXiv 2611.26983

Review finding. The paper maps 22 mechanisms/primitives across parameter, prompt, preference, memory, skill, and cross-carrier updates. Its worked scenarios show why reader non-use does not prove cleanup of learned state and recommend write–query–delete–probe–recovery–re-ingestion testing.

Limits. This is a thematic review with worked scenarios, not an integrated production implementation. The screened corpus ends 10 November 2026 and contains uneven evidence across carriers.

Why it matters. Deletion from a vector store can leave derived skills, preferences, or weights influenced by the withdrawn fact.

TPM application (proposed). Build a state-carrier inventory and dependency graph for each memory write, with a synchronized withdrawal job and evidence per carrier.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Cross-carrier withdrawal could improve defensibility of deletion claims but may increase storage lineage, retraining, and recovery complexity.

Enterprise privacyPreprint · formal design + prototype

4. Lineage-gated memory blocks reuse of results derived from forbidden columns

Venkata M. Sangaraju & Sudhir Vissa · 5 October 2026 · arXiv 2610.07258

Formal and measured. Under complete recorded lineage, the retrieval gate prevents returning cached results derived from columns outside the requester’s permission set. In six experiments, naïve content-gated memory leaked in 18.8–25.5% of cross-department cases; lineage gating retained 81.5–82.6% reuse with 13.8 μs reported worst-case overhead. Measured leakage reached zero only when lineage completeness was 75–90%, depending on schema.

Limits. The guarantee is conditional on complete lineage. The extractor has gaps for views, stored procedures, deep aliasing, and undeclared sensitive derived features; the real-agent test was only nine round trips.

Why it matters. Content-only access controls cannot see the sensitive inputs embedded in an innocuous cached metric.

TPM application (proposed). Attach column-level derivation and a definition hash to shared analytical memory; fail closed when lineage coverage is uncertain.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Derivation-aware reuse may reduce cross-team leakage and metric conflicts, but incomplete telemetry becomes an assurance-critical dependency.

Behavioral privacyPreprint · simulation

5. Memory can create a self-reinforcing disclosure loop

Masahiro Kaneko, Hiromi Arai & Timothy Baldwin · 20 October 2026 · arXiv 2610.28315

Measured. In multi-session simulations, a user with 24 personal attributes interacts with support agents with or without memory. Across four support models, memory improves response quality but increases the share of attributes inferable from the accumulated record; removing agent mentions of an attribute lowers later disclosure about twice as much as the single-turn effect predicts.

Limits. Simulated users and support tasks do not reproduce every human response, culture, domain, or product interface. Attribute inference is a behavioral proxy, not proof of database exfiltration.

Why it matters. The agent’s own personalization can elicit or entrench data the user did not intend to restate.

TPM application (proposed). Test “do not state or presuppose sensitive attributes” as a response-layer control, then measure quality and longitudinal disclosure.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Response shaping may preserve more personalization value than blunt storage removal, but it must complement—not replace—retention and deletion controls.

AI security governanceOfficial NIST initial public draft

6. NIST adds an agentic attack taxonomy to adversarial ML guidance

NIST, Northeastern University, ElevenLabs & U.K. AI Security Institute · 9 November 2026 · NIST SP 2400-102 E2026 · Comments due 18 December 2026

Authoritative taxonomy, not measured efficacy. The draft extends adversarial-ML terminology to agentic systems, covering prompt injection, poisoning, tool-mediated actions, memory/context attacks, inter-agent communication, cascading failure, identity/privilege abuse, and corresponding mitigation concepts.

Limits. It is an initial public draft, voluntary guidance, and a taxonomy—not evidence that any mapped control works in a specific deployment.

Why it matters. A common vocabulary can connect threat modeling, test cases, incidents, and executive risk reporting.

TPM application (proposed). Map each deployed agent’s threat model and red-team suite to the draft categories; submit gaps or ambiguous definitions during the comment window.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Early mapping may reduce future control-remediation work and improve audit consistency, but the final publication may change.

Continual learningPreprint

7. Self-evolving agents expose a stability–plasticity governance problem

Research team · 19 October 2026 · arXiv 2610.25858 · PDF

Measured. Three self-evolution methods are compared over four non-stationary task streams. Aggregate performance improves, but methods trade off learning new tasks against retaining old behavior; foreign or conflicting memories can introduce regressions.

Limits. Four controlled streams do not establish open-world reliability, safety, or long-run improvement. Aggregate gains can hide task-specific forgetting.

Why it matters. A continuously changing agent invalidates a one-time approval unless memory and policy changes are versioned and re-evaluated.

TPM application (proposed). Require pre-promotion retention, privacy, and security regression suites for each learned-state update, plus checkpoint rollback.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Controlled adaptation may reduce manual tuning; unmanaged adaptation can raise incident variance and evidence drift.

Evaluation governancePreprint

8. User-simulator metrics can certify a flawed evaluator

Research team · 18 November 2026 · arXiv 2611.24892

Measured. In a meta-evaluation with ten seeded simulator defects, prior metrics identify only five to six; the proposed suite detects all ten. The study also examines 351 sessions from 348 users across 23 languages.

Limits. Seeded defects are controlled proxies, and the human dataset is not evidence for every domain, agent, culture, or simulator design.

Why it matters. A synthetic-user launch gate is only as reliable as the simulator and its evaluation.

TPM application (proposed). Qualify the simulator with planted failures and a small privacy-reviewed human comparison before using it as release evidence.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Evaluator qualification may reduce false launches and false blocks, at the cost of more upfront benchmark governance.

Executable governanceAccepted workshop paper

9. Agent Contracts turn selected invariants into enforceable gates

Research team · 13 January 2026; revised 25 March · arXiv 2601.08815

Measured. Across four experiments, the contract approach reports zero conservation violations, 90% lower token use, and 525× lower variance than the studied prompt-based baseline on selected resource-bounded tasks.

Limits. The experiments cover specific environments and invariants. Misspecified contracts, incomplete coverage, integration errors, and incorrect external state remain risks.

Why it matters. Policy text alone is difficult to test and enforce consistently at tool-execution time.

TPM application (proposed). Encode one high-severity rule—data egress, spending, or approval authority—as a pre/postcondition with test vectors and logged denial evidence.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Executable controls may reduce behavioral variance and audit collection time, while shifting risk into policy formalization and coverage assurance.

Agent capability evaluationPreprint

10. Visual Cipher Bench isolates autonomous rule discovery as a bottleneck

Research team · 12 November 2026; revised 16 November · arXiv 2611.16861

Measured. The benchmark contains 40 expert-verified puzzles, 67 images, eight frontier models, 29 reasoning configurations, and 3,480 attempts in a network-disabled sandbox. The best configuration reaches 26.7% accuracy; pooled rule-induction accuracy is 0.6%. On a ten-task ablation, a mechanism hint raises accuracy from 7.8% to 55.6% and cuts timeouts from 51 to 18.

Limits. Puzzle accuracy is not enterprise task success. The ablation is small, and proprietary model/version changes can alter rankings.

Why it matters. Long tool traces do not imply that an agent has correctly inferred the governing rule.

TPM application (proposed). Separate rule-discovery, plan execution, and recovery metrics in agent evaluations; provide bounded hints only when the operating policy allows them.

Business hypothesis / forward look. Decomposed diagnostics may improve routing and escalation decisions, but benchmark gains need domain validation.

Top impactful research and applications

Strong emerging audit evidence FORGETMENOT — 22 Nov 2026

Method: black-box, multi-turn privacy audit of five commercial agents with ordinary, adversarial, deletion, and third-party disclosure tests. Evidence strength: large behavioral sample and concrete leakage measurements. Limitation: no internal storage visibility; products evolve. Operational application: recurring memory transparency and deletion assurance. Expected impact (hypothesis): earlier detection of privacy regressions and more defensible user-control claims.

Broad benchmark contribution HEAMS — 15 Nov 2026

Method: open-source behavioral benchmark plus a governance wrapper spanning performance, efficiency, security, privacy, and sovereignty. Evidence strength: cross-objective comparison rather than one retrieval score. Limitation: incomplete benchmark coverage and no production field validation. Operational application: memory vendor selection and release gates. Expected impact (hypothesis): fewer unsafe “best performer” selections.

Formal design with scoped experiments Lineage-aware memory governance — 5 Oct 2026

Method: derivation-carrying analytical memory, permission gate, definition hashes, formal proof, six experiments, and a small real-agent prototype. Evidence strength: explicit assumptions and measured completeness degradation. Limitation: incomplete lineage and hidden derived features break the protection boundary. Operational application: shared analytical memory across business units. Expected impact (hypothesis): reduced cross-boundary leakage with continued safe reuse.

Useful longitudinal mechanism study Disclosure Begets Disclosure — 20 Oct 2026

Method: multi-session simulation across four models and 24 personal attributes, with response-level interventions. Evidence strength: isolates a reinforcing behavioral mechanism over time. Limitation: simulated users. Operational application: sensitive-attribute presupposition testing. Expected impact (hypothesis): personalization with less induced disclosure.

Important continual-learning evidence Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners? — 19 Oct 2026

Method: three evolution methods across four drifting streams. Evidence strength: controlled comparison of adaptation and retention. Limitation: limited task ecology and duration. Operational application: versioned memory promotion with regression and rollback. Expected impact (hypothesis): safer capture of adaptation gains without treating change as automatically beneficial.

Research to Build

One-week prototype · Agent Memory Withdrawal Assurance Harness

Prove what “delete” actually removes—and what still influences behavior

Status: proposed enterprise experiment inspired by FORGETMENOT, HEAMS, and the cross-carrier privacy review. None of those papers validates this combined control in your environment.

Seed state.
Create synthetic identities with unique canaries across health, contact, location, preference, and restricted-work attributes. Exercise chat, explicit memory, retrieved documents, and learned skills.
Map carriers.
Capture writes to chat history, extracted memory, embeddings, caches, summaries, skills, profiles, logs, and backups. Attach purpose, sensitivity, lineage, owner, and TTL.
Withdraw and attack.
Delete through the supported user path, then probe direct recall, paraphrase, tool arguments, personalization, third-party actions, recovery, rollback, and re-ingestion.
Gate the claim.
Require no exact canary disclosure, bounded semantic influence, completed dependency closure, replayable evidence, explicit residual-risk owner, and an expiry date.

Metrics: visible-to-observed memory coverage, exact and semantic leakage, post-deletion influence delta, propagation completion time, carrier coverage, rollback/recovery leakage, false deletion confidence, task-quality delta, and evidence replay success.

Important interpretation: “No disclosure observed” is not certified erasure. Label logical invalidation, physical deletion, and non-reconstructability separately, and state the tested adversary and carriers.

Near-term signals to watch

1 · Independent commercial-memory replication

Whether vendor updates or third-party studies reproduce the FORGETMENOT transparency, deletion, and recipient-disclosure findings.

2 · Memory benchmark standardization

Convergence on tests for inappropriate recall, poisoning, user inspection, correction, withdrawal, and cross-carrier propagation—not only retrieval accuracy.

3 · NIST agentic taxonomy feedback

Clarifications before the December 18 comment deadline, especially on memory/context poisoning, authorization scope, incident evidence, and inter-agent risk.

One practical action

Run one synthetic write–inspect–delete–probe sequence against your highest-risk agent this week. Record every state carrier, compare what the user can see with what behavior reveals, and refuse to call the control “deletion” until downstream influence and recovery paths have been tested.
