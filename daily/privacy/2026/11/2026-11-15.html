Daily evidence brief · Privacy Engineering + AI Governance

Agent learning creates a new control plane

Today’s strongest papers converge on a practical rule: memory, tool updates and multi-agent coordination cannot inherit trust silently. Learning must preserve provenance and deletion lineage; changed tools require reauthorization; and performance gains must be paired with stability, attack and recourse evidence before autonomy expands.

November 15, 2026

Executive read

Three decisions for leaders

  1. Treat memory as authority-bearing data. Every stored lesson, permission and derived summary needs provenance, scope, expiry and deletion lineage.
  2. Reauthorize change, not just admission. A tool that was safe when approved is a new risk object after code, dependency, permission or endpoint changes.
  3. Gate learning on stability. Report plasticity, forgetting, cross-task interference and security regression beside aggregate task lift.

10 high-signal developments

Five privacy/security and five governance/reliability developments, selected for methodological specificity and direct enterprise-control implications.

Privacy 1/5Preprint · tool supply chain

Trusted tools can become hostile after an update

Shiyu Kan, Yue Qi, Yufei Han, Xinjian Luo, Guojun Xiong, Xiaofeng Gao · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.01976

Measured. ETUDE embeds malicious functionality in updates to initially benign tools while preserving legitimate behavior. Across three attack workloads and seven commercial or open-source agent frameworks, the paper reports 97.6% attack success, 95.9% legitimate task completion and an 85.1-point advantage over its strongest prompt-injection baseline.
Limits. The study evaluates selected frameworks and workloads, not every updater, sandbox or code-signing architecture. Attack success assumes the adversary can influence a trusted update path; production dependency controls may differ.
Why it matters
Admission-time approval does not cover later code, dependency or permission changes outside the model’s context.
TPM / engineering application
Bind tool identity to content and dependency hashes; require delta review, permission diff and behavioral tests before restoring trust after an update.
Business outcome hypothesis
Continuous reauthorization may reduce persistent agent compromise and improve software-supply-chain auditability.
Forward implication
Agent registries will need version-specific attestations rather than one durable “approved” flag.

Governance 1/5Preprint · continual learning

Self-evolution improves task averages while eroding stability

Xiaoyin Chen, Yizheng Huang, Yinglun Xu, Ziyun Zhao, Emmett McQuinn, Julie Liu, Hope Yao · 19 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.25858

Measured. Three frozen-weight methods—GEPA, ReasoningBank and Meta-Harness—were tested across four drifting streams built from BIRD, BFCL and ScienceWorld. Average terminal advantage was +3.7, +2.5 and +1.4 points respectively. GEPA had the highest average plasticity (+6.2) but largest stability loss (−4.3); ReasoningBank was more stable (−1.9) but retrieved the opposite BFCL workflow for 52% and 46% of two task groups at the final checkpoint.
Limits. Results use one base model and synthetic benchmark streams; the metrics do not establish real-world safety. Harness implementations, retrieval thresholds and task ordering can change the trade-off.
Why it matters
Aggregate improvement can conceal interference, overwriting and retrieval collision on earlier or unseen work.
TPM / engineering application
Add stability, forgetting, foreign-memory retrieval and pre-block interference to every self-improvement release gate.
Business outcome hypothesis
Regression-aware learning may prevent quality gains in one workflow from creating support cost or risk elsewhere.
Forward implication
Agent change reviews will resemble continual-learning safety cases, not prompt-only approvals.

Privacy 2/5Preprint · deletion audit

Deleting a source record does not delete its descendants

Ehsan Saleh, Roozbeh Nahavandi, Sama Salarian, Raef Bassily, Andrew Perrault · 22 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.31366

Measured. AfterDeleteBench compares agents exposed to a synthetic secret and then deleted with matched never-exposed controls. A single query recovered the secret from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in roughly two-thirds of primary-setting cases while controls recovered none. With no full secret stored, composition from surviving components succeeded in 62–99% of cases across Qwen, Mistral and Phi-3. Provenance-aware closure eliminated observed recovery in tested positive-baseline settings.
Limits. Synthetic secrets, fixed-weight agents and tested memory designs do not cover all summarizers, vector stores or model retraining. “No observed recovery” is not a proof of erasure.
Why it matters
Deletion must propagate through summaries, indexes, components, caches and write-backs—not stop at the originating row.
TPM / engineering application
Maintain a derivation graph and run exposed-versus-never-exposed behavioral tests after deletion closure.
Business outcome hypothesis
Provenance-aware deletion could reduce regulatory exposure and strengthen customer deletion evidence.
Forward implication
Agent deletion SLAs will include behavioral non-recovery, not only storage confirmation.

Governance 2/5Preprint · public-sector recourse

Visibility is not recourse: notice, challenge and correction remain separate controls

Snehalkumar “Neil” S. Gaikwad, Punya Aragula, Shiva Kruthi Palpanoori, Pratik Patekar · 12 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.16173

Measured / documentary. The Society-Centered AI Governance Framework was applied through interviews with nine North Carolina AI Leadership Council members and human-reviewed, model-assisted coding of 49 governance documents from 19 states. For North Carolina’s own AI systems, visibility duties were in force, notice existed as an agency duty without an invocable individual right, and AI-specific challenge and correction remained advisory.
Limits. The analysis is jurisdiction-specific and partly documentary; it does not measure downstream harm or whether roadmap commitments will be implemented. Coding and legal interpretation remain contestable.
Why it matters
A register can make AI visible without giving an affected person a route to correct an outcome.
TPM / engineering application
Map each use case to notice, challenge, correction, owner, binding authority and evidence of remedy completion.
Business outcome hypothesis
Recourse-ready workflows may reduce complaint friction and improve defensibility in public-sector deployments.
Forward implication
Governance assurance will test whether a person can act on disclosures, not just whether disclosures exist.

Privacy 3/5Preprint · authorization memory

Agent memory can launder authority that history never granted

Tommaso Cerruti, Mika Okamoto, Ansel Kaplan Erol · 1 Sep 2026 · arXiv:2609.01836

Measured. EAL-Bench evaluates five LLM memory writers and two executors across procurement, cybersecurity and finance. With incremental memory updates, writers created false authority for up to 50.2% of unauthorized requests; once false authority appeared in memory, executors acted on it in 98.6% of trials. Source-backed permissions and bounded event sourcing reduced laundering but rejected more legitimate actions.
Limits. “Up to” results depend on the model, scenario and update policy. The benchmark does not prove either safeguard is sufficient for production identity or authorization systems.
Why it matters
Summarized permission state can silently become the effective policy engine.
TPM / engineering application
Resolve permissions from signed source events at action time; do not authorize from free-text memory summaries.
Business outcome hypothesis
Event-sourced authority may reduce unauthorized actions and make access decisions easier to audit.
Forward implication
Long-lived agent memory will be reviewed as part of IAM architecture, not merely personalization.

Governance 3/5Preprint · system reliability

More agents do not automatically make a more reliable system

Burkay Tunçtürk, Koorosh Aslansefat, Yiannis Papadopoulos, Bao Nguyen · 4 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.07140

Measured / modeled. The paper propagates uncertain single-agent failure intervals through a Bayesian failure network for a regulated chemical-hazard extraction pipeline. Majority voting among three models protected against the weakest agent but did not clearly beat the best agent. At a 5% voter-failure probability, redundancy no longer improved even on the middle agent at the lower reliability bound.
Limits. Reliability envelopes depend on operational-profile assumptions, conservative dependence modeling and one pipeline. A modeled common-cause factor is not a measured production rate.
Why it matters
Coordinator and common-cause failures can erase the expected benefit of agent redundancy.
TPM / engineering application
Model voter failure, correlated errors and production input mix before claiming multi-agent reliability gains.
Business outcome hypothesis
System-level reliability cases may avoid unnecessary agent cost and overconfident assurance claims.
Forward implication
Multi-agent approvals will require configuration-level evidence, not component benchmark averages.

Privacy 4/5Accepted workshop paper · private retrieval

Signed semantic memory and private retrieval can reduce tampering and query disclosure

Vicenç Torra, Maria Bras-Amorós · 10 Nov 2026 · accepted ESORICS 2026 DPM workshop; arXiv:2611.13696

Implemented. A signed Prolog-like knowledge base combines provenance with bucket, k-anonymous and two-server information-theoretic private retrieval. On 18 queries over 99 facts/rules (46 database entries), five-run mean uncached execution was 0.178 seconds for baseline, 0.179–0.181 seconds for k-anonymity configurations and 0.223 seconds for two-server PIR.
Limits. This is a small structured knowledge base, not a neural vector store or adversarial end-to-end agent evaluation. Signatures authenticate origin but do not guarantee truth, appropriateness or non-malicious signed content.
Why it matters
Memory integrity and retrieval privacy are distinct: one protects content provenance, the other protects what the agent asks for.
TPM / engineering application
Prototype signed memory objects and hide sensitive retrieval intent where the query itself reveals private workflow context.
Business outcome hypothesis
Private retrieval may strengthen cross-tenant knowledge services with measurable latency overhead.
Forward implication
Enterprise agent memory will combine cryptographic provenance with query-privacy choices by risk tier.

Governance 4/5Preprint · trace diagnosis

Trace decomposition improves failure localization over monolithic judging

Netta Madvil et al. · 14 May 2026 · arXiv:2605.14865

Measured. A top-down agent assessment plus bottom-up span evaluation was tested on TRAIL’s GAIA and SWE-bench traces. Against the strongest prior baselines, the paper reports gains up to 38% in category F1, 3.5× in localization accuracy and 12.5× in joint localization-plus-categorization. The same frontier model achieved 2.8× higher localization on GAIA and 12× on SWE-bench inside the framework than as a monolithic judge.
Limits. Benchmark labels and evaluator models may not transfer to private workflows; a more accurate diagnosis is not proof that remediation will work or that the judge is unbiased.
Why it matters
Pass/fail outcomes do not identify which prompt, model call, tool result or policy transition caused the failure.
TPM / engineering application
Store structured traces and require span-level localization plus trace-level failure categories for critical regressions.
Business outcome hypothesis
Better localization may shorten root-cause analysis and reduce repeated release failures.
Forward implication
Agent observability platforms will pair outcome metrics with causal-looking but contestable diagnostic evidence.

Privacy 5/5Preprint · adaptive prompt injection

Adaptive attacker swarms reopen “solved” prompt-injection benchmarks

Alexey Kurakin et al. · 9 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.11861

Measured. Agentic Optimizer uses communicating attacker teams to mutate indirect-injection payloads. With out-of-model mitigations disabled, the strongest attacker left Gemini 4 Argon resisting 87% of scenarios after 150 queries and 66% at the maximum budget; several other evaluated models resisted only 1–34% at 150 queries. On 80 AgentDojo scenarios with deployed defenses, the adaptive method succeeded in 6 cases within 25 queries versus 2 for the comparison attack.
Limits. Main-table Gemini tests disabled external mitigations and used synthetic unrelated tool-call goals. Large adaptive budgets and model versions may not reflect every production threat or cost constraint.
Why it matters
Static and transfer-attack scores can overstate resilience when the attacker adapts to the current defender.
TPM / engineering application
Add bounded adaptive red teaming and full-stack mitigations to every consequential agent release; record query budget and defense configuration.
Business outcome hypothesis
Adaptive testing may uncover bypasses before scale while focusing defense investment on realistic failure paths.
Forward implication
Security scorecards will state attacker capability, budget and visibility—not simply “prompt-injection pass rate.”

Governance 5/5Preprint · execution control

Policy enforcement at the execution boundary can outperform prompt-only governance

Tianyu Shi, Yang Mo, Yiou Liu, Zhuonan Hao, Yin Wang, Wenzhuo Hu, Nan Yu, Meng Zhou, Jiangbo Yu · 3 Jun 2026 · arXiv:2606.04306

Measured. The Organizational Control Layer intercepts model-generated actions for policy enforcement and escalation without modifying the generator. In adversarial buyer–seller environments across multiple frontier backends, the paper reports unsafe executions falling from 88% to near zero while valid success rose from 12% to 96%.
Limits. Negotiation simulations are narrower than enterprise production. “Near zero” is not zero, and strict rules reduced flexibility in constrained markets; policy completeness and enforcement integrity remain assumptions.
Why it matters
Generated intent and permission to execute should be separate decisions at consequential boundaries.
TPM / engineering application
Place deterministic policy, budget and escalation checks between agent proposals and every state-changing API.
Business outcome hypothesis
Execution-boundary control may reduce unsafe actions while preserving useful automation.
Forward implication
Agent governance will move from prompt guidance toward independently owned runtime control planes.

Research to Build

Two-week control experiment

Continual-learning safety gate for agent harness changes

Turn every prompt, memory, skill or tool update into a controlled change evaluated for task lift, stability, privacy, authority and supply-chain integrity.

Protocol

  1. Create four 50-task streams spanning distinct workflows plus one pair of similar queries that require opposite tool behavior.
  2. Compare a frozen baseline, global self-modification and provenance-scoped retrieval; keep model, tools and sampling fixed.
  3. At every checkpoint, replay all prior groups and unseen groups; record task gain, pre-block interference, post-block forgetting and foreign-memory retrieval.
  4. Inject permission grants and revocations; verify that action-time authorization resolves from signed source events rather than summaries.
  5. Introduce a benign-looking tool update with a changed dependency or permission; require reauthorization before execution.
  6. Delete one synthetic secret and its source, then probe summaries, fragments, indexes and fresh sessions for recovery.

Decision package

  • Utility: terminal and average advantage by workflow.
  • Retention: stability, forgetting and reversal rates.
  • Privacy: post-deletion recovery and cross-scope leakage.
  • Authority: false grants and unauthorized executions.
  • Supply chain: update-diff detection and blocked stale attestations.
  • Operations: benign blocks, reviewer load and p95 latency.

Decision-rule hypothesis: promote a change only when it improves its target workflow, stays within locally chosen stability and privacy thresholds, creates no critical unauthorized action, and reauthorizes every changed tool artifact. The papers do not establish universal thresholds.

Three near-term signals to watch

01

Adaptive attacks against full stacks

Whether swarm-generated injections retain their advantage when external classifiers, capability fences and runtime authorization are enabled together.

02

Deletion closure beyond external memory

Whether provenance-aware deletion remains effective across embeddings, summaries, caches, fine-tuning data and model-derived state.

03

Stability metrics in agent platforms

Whether vendors expose forgetting, interference, foreign-memory retrieval and update provenance as first-class operational evidence.

One practical action

Add a “changed trust surface” checklist to your next agent release

For each changed prompt, memory policy, skill, model, tool, dependency or permission, record its source and hash, replay scope, deletion impact, authorization impact, required reapproval and rollback owner. Treat any unassessed surface as a release blocker—not as inherited trust.
