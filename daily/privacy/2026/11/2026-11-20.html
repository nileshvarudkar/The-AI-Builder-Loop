Privacy Engineering &
AI Governance

Today’s thesis: outcome-only assurance is too weak for persistent agents. Privacy and governance controls must examine the full trajectory—what an agent accessed, retained, changed, and transmitted—and prove that apparent learning survives interference, drift, and adversarial evidence.

Executive readout

Four findings align: realistic agent privacy testing reveals large leakage that final-answer scoring misses; cross-framework MCP testing finds real secret exfiltration; persistent harnesses frequently fail to learn beyond simple caching; and one-time fairness audits expire when hosted models change. The operational pattern is to version the whole execution context and gate access, memory, action, and outbound flow separately.

10 high-signal developments

Privacy & security · 01

Executable agent privacy audits cut measured leakage, but do not eliminate it

Shouju Wang & Haopeng Zhang · 1 Oct 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2610.06454

Measured finding
AgentPrivArena ran 389 executable tasks with authentic MCP tools and five executors. Pooled leak rate was 46.8% without mitigation, 43.0% with a privacy prompt, and 17.8% with contextual-integrity auditing; helpfulness moved from 2.60 to 2.59 on a 0–3 scale.
Method & limitations
The sandbox used self-hosted services, 1,175 protected items and three external judges. It converted 389 of 493 PrivacyLens scenarios; the auditor was GPT-5.4, so transfer to other policies, auditors and production services is unproven.
Why it matters
Privacy prompting alone barely changed leakage; policy-aware interception at read and write boundaries produced the larger measured effect.
TPM application
Instrument tool trajectories, maintain a protected-data flow inventory, and gate outbound writes with configurable PII, minimization and contextual-integrity criteria.
Business outcome hypothesis
Trajectory controls may reduce accidental disclosure while preserving task utility, but add latency, inference cost and auditor dependence.
Forward implication
Enterprise agent privacy reviews will increasingly require executable traces, not only final-response samples.
Privacy & security · 02

Real MCP tool poisoning is partly model-driven and survives framework changes

Farooq Shaikh & Biagio Montaruli · revised 9 Nov 2026 · Peer-reviewed workshop paper · arXiv 2611.03555

Measured finding
A 9,720-cell matrix across 12 frameworks, 18 models and 45 attack scenarios produced 2,113 vulnerable cells (22.9%). Among vulnerable cells, 64.9% dispatched an attacker call, 23.0% contacted an attacker endpoint and 12.1% verifiably exfiltrated a secret.
Method & limitations
A dual oracle confirmed canary HTTP receipt. Nine core frameworks clustered at 23–26% ASR (p=.91); model ASR ranged 0.4–49.7%. Synthetic canaries and tested frameworks do not quantify production incidence.
Why it matters
Changing orchestration frameworks is not a reliable substitute for model- and host-level defenses.
TPM application
Require signed tool metadata, egress allowlists, user-visible approval for new destinations, and canary-based execution testing.
Business outcome hypothesis
Host-controlled egress may reduce exfiltration risk and audit ambiguity, with integration cost for dynamic tools.
Forward implication
MCP security baselines will likely treat tool descriptions and schemas as untrusted code-adjacent inputs.
Privacy & security · 03

“Log-to-Leak” hides unauthorized disclosure behind successful work

Yuepeng Hu et al. · 26 Oct 2026 · Peer-reviewed workshop paper · arXiv 2610.36108

Measured finding
Across 18 valid model-server pairs, an injected post-hoc logging call achieved 62.64–100% attack success and 76.55–95.64% conditional logging accuracy. Sixteen pairs changed legitimate task completion by no more than 6.63 percentage points.
Method & limitations
Five MCP servers and four agents were tested with separate call-success, payload-fidelity and utility metrics. Three defenses still left residual ASR above 55% for one GitHub MCP/GPT-4o setting; coverage beyond tested servers is unknown.
Why it matters
High task success can coexist with invisible secondary disclosure.
TPM application
Make every post-task or secondary tool call a new authorization event; block destinations not present in the original execution plan.
Business outcome hypothesis
Secondary-call controls may reduce data loss without materially reducing primary task completion.
Forward implication
Agent success metrics will need an “unauthorized extra action” dimension.
Privacy & security · 04

Double-blind safety evaluations become technically feasible

Andrew Trask et al. · 9 Nov 2026 · Preprint / pilot report · arXiv 2611.12604

Measured finding
A pilot evaluated proprietary Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite against a private subset of the AILuminate benchmark inside an NVIDIA H100 secure enclave on Google Cloud, using PySyft to keep benchmark prompts and model assets mutually confidential.
Method & limitations
The paper demonstrates one end-to-end pilot; it does not report broad performance, side-channel resistance, cost or multi-cloud portability. “First” is the authors’ claim, not an independently verified market survey.
Why it matters
Evaluators can protect benchmark secrecy while model owners protect weights and inference code.
TPM application
Pilot confidential-compute evaluation for red-team suites that cannot be shared with providers or product teams.
Business outcome hypothesis
Mutual secrecy could increase external audit participation and extend benchmark shelf life, subject to enclave assurance and operational cost.
Forward implication
High-stakes assurance may move from document exchange to attested evaluation environments.
Privacy & security · 05

Curated forgetting can reduce poisoned-memory risk and device cost

B. Wu et al. · 23 Jun 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2606.25115

Measured finding
CURATOR reports 97% of a full-memory oracle’s accuracy at 37% of its footprint, 2.7× lower peak memory, 2.4× lower per-round uplink and injection success reduced from 0.75 to zero on tested task-drift benchmarks and a Jetson robot-arm testbed.
Method & limitations
Experiments used three backbones, two robot-arm nodes and a hub. The net-value score and poisoning setup may not transfer to enterprise text agents, longer horizons or adaptive attackers.
Why it matters
Retention minimization can improve security, efficiency and freshness simultaneously in a measured setting.
TPM application
Score memories by verified usefulness, freshness, provenance and risk; evict low-net-value entries under explicit storage and communication budgets.
Business outcome hypothesis
Selective retention may lower compute and breach exposure without reducing quality, if value scoring remains calibrated.
Forward implication
Agent memory governance will treat deletion as an optimization primitive, not only a compliance obligation.
Governance & reliability · 06

Persistent harnesses often memorize less than a simple cache

Daniel Lenton, Yusha Arif & Haris Mahmood · revised 15 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.14505

Measured finding
Continual-ARC streams 200 recurring task instances, then freezes learners and tests 100 unseen tasks. Most harness methods learned less than caching demonstrations; weight-trained Qwen3.5-9B spent 29% less than its untrained self on unseen tasks (95% interval 23–35%).
Method & limitations
Frozen-weight Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash harnesses were compared with prompting, program induction and DAgger weight training. ARC is one abstract domain, and many runs used relatively small models.
Why it matters
Persistence is not evidence of learning; it may only store task-specific artifacts.
TPM application
Separate retention tests on recurring tasks from transfer tests on unseen tasks and compare against a trivial cache baseline.
Business outcome hypothesis
Baseline-gated evaluation can prevent expensive “learning” features that do not generalize.
Forward implication
Continual-agent claims will need dormant-gap retention and unseen-task transfer evidence.
Governance & reliability · 07

Self-evolving agents still trade plasticity for stability

Xiaoyin Chen et al. · 19 Oct 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2610.25858

Measured finding
Across four drifting streams built from BIRD, BFCL and ScienceWorld, ReasoningBank, GEPA and Meta-Harness generally improved aggregate performance, but more plastic methods interfered with unseen tasks and overwrote earlier knowledge; retrieval systems suffered foreign-memory and workflow-collision errors.
Method & limitations
Three frozen-weight self-evolution approaches were tested across topic, workflow and domain drift. Results do not establish a universal stability–plasticity frontier across proprietary agents or production workloads.
Why it matters
Aggregate gains can hide regression on old and unseen tasks.
TPM application
Gate every prompt, skill or memory update on backward-regression, unseen-transfer and collision tests.
Business outcome hypothesis
Retention-aware release gates may reduce silent workflow breakage, at the cost of larger regression suites.
Forward implication
Self-evolution will be governed like continuous deployment, with canaries, rollback and provenance.
Governance & reliability · 08

Fairness evidence expires when provider-served models move

Mika Facchini, Technical University of Munich · 16 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.20901

Measured finding
Across 248,400 API calls, five providers and three audits over 13 months, the same model identifier showed a significantly different bias pattern one year later; successor models retained bias in changed size and form. Provider notice periods ranged from zero to six months.
Method & limitations
Models made venture-screening decisions for profiles varied by gender, origin and university. Findings may not generalize to every domain or fairness construct; the paper is observational, not a causal model-update audit.
Why it matters
A passed audit can become stale without any application-code change.
TPM application
Record exact model/version metadata per response and trigger re-audits on alias, weight, provider or serving changes.
Business outcome hypothesis
Expiry-aware evidence can reduce false compliance confidence and remediation surprises.
Forward implication
Audit artifacts will need validity windows and model-class coverage, not one-time certificates.
Governance & reliability · 09

Incident-response agents detect threats but fail to exercise restraint

Jarrod Barnes · revised 6 Feb 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2601.21083

Measured finding
In 40 standard-tier episodes per model, GPT-5.2 contained in 100% of episodes with an 82.5% false-positive rate, often acting at step four. Claude Sonnet 4.5 contained in 62.5%, with 45% false positives and mean time-to-first-containment of 10.6 steps.
Method & limitations
OpenSec uses execution scoring under prompt injection and reports evidence-gated action, blast radius and tiered violations. Forty episodes per model and a synthetic RL environment limit precision and field generalization.
Why it matters
Correct threat recognition does not imply calibrated authority to act.
TPM application
Separate detection from execution permission; require evidence thresholds, reversible containment and blast-radius budgets.
Business outcome hypothesis
Restraint gates may reduce costly false containment while slowing true response in edge cases.
Forward implication
Autonomous security-agent governance will emphasize abstention and evidence-gated action.
Governance & reliability · 10

Agent memory needs governance metrics beyond recall

Chirag Goel et al. · 15 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.20144

Measured finding
HEAMS evaluates memory across performance, efficiency, security, privacy and user sovereignty. The authors report that no memory system leads every performance benchmark; high task performance can coexist with weak privacy and sovereignty, while recall restrictions trade helpfulness for lower inappropriate disclosure.
Method & limitations
The 69-page study assembles multiple benchmarks, a prototype memory system and a wrapper governance layer. The abstract does not supply a single cross-system numeric effect, and the suite acknowledges coverage gaps.
Why it matters
Recall accuracy is an incomplete success criterion for persistent user information.
TPM application
Add deletion compliance, retention expiry, poisoning resistance, inappropriate disclosure and user-control tests to memory release criteria.
Business outcome hypothesis
Holistic memory scorecards may reduce privacy incidents and improve customer trust, with possible helpfulness tradeoffs.
Forward implication
Memory components will acquire their own assurance cases, comparable to models and data stores.

Top impactful research and applications

Five studies with the clearest operational path. Applications and impacts remain hypotheses unless the cited work tested them directly.

AgentPrivArena — preprint, 1 Oct 2026

Evidence quality: 389 executable tasks, five executors, authentic tools, explicit trajectory metrics and three independent judges.

Limit: benchmark conversion, one auditor model and self-hosted services.

Plausible application: privacy CI/CD that blocks agent releases when protected data is unnecessarily accessed or transmitted.

Expected impact hypothesis: stronger evidence of minimization and purpose limitation than final-output testing alone.

Continual-ARC — preprint, revised 15 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: matched feedback budgets, recurring tasks after dormant gaps, frozen post-stream tests and a trivial-cache baseline.

Limit: one abstract task family and smaller models.

Plausible application: a “learning claim gate” separating retention, generalization and mere retrieval.

Expected impact hypothesis: fewer false claims that persistent memory equals continual learning.

MCP tool-poisoning measurement — peer-reviewed workshop paper, revised 9 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: 9,720 tested cells and real canary exfiltration across 12 frameworks and 18 models.

Limit: synthetic attacks and no production base rate.

Plausible application: MCP admission testing, signed metadata and destination-level egress enforcement.

Expected impact hypothesis: fewer tool-supply-chain and exfiltration incidents.

Auditing a Moving Target — preprint, 16 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: 248,400 API calls across five providers and three audits over 13 months.

Limit: venture screening and observational model changes.

Plausible application: evidence-expiry metadata and provider-change-triggered re-audits.

Expected impact hypothesis: fewer stale fairness attestations.

HEAMS — preprint, 15 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: a broad open-source benchmark spanning utility, privacy, security and user sovereignty, plus a wrapper governance layer.

Limit: incomplete coverage and policy-dependent tradeoffs.

Plausible application: memory-system acceptance criteria that include deletion, poisoning and disclosure—not only recall.

Expected impact hypothesis: more governable long-lived enterprise agents.

Research to Build: Trajectory Assurance Harness

Goal: test whether a persistent enterprise agent improves safely over time, without leaking protected data, retaining stale or poisoned memories, or regressing old workflows.

Prototype

  1. Select 30 recurring privacy-review or remediation tasks and 20 unseen transfer tasks.
  2. Run reset, simple-cache and persistent/self-editing variants under identical model, tool and token budgets.
  3. Seed ten failure classes: prompt injection, poisoned memory, stale policy, wrong-user memory, ambiguous object, excess read, unauthorized write, workflow collision, deletion failure and version drift.
  4. Record every read, memory write, retrieval, tool proposal, authorization decision and outbound transmission.

Evaluation and gate

  • Learning: dormant-gap retention, unseen-task transfer and regression versus reset and cache baselines.
  • Privacy: protected-item exposure, unnecessary access, outbound leakage and deletion compliance.
  • Reliability: wrong-action rate, evidence-gated action rate and rollback success.
  • Promotion: no critical leak or unauthorized action in seeded cases; statistically bounded regression; every mutation versioned and reversible.

Evidence boundary This is a proposed enterprise experiment. The selected papers motivate its controls but do not prove operational effectiveness in Azure privacy workflows.

Three near-term signals to watch

1 · Trajectory standardsWhether agent assurance frameworks begin requiring access, memory and transmission traces as first-class evidence.
2 · Model-change triggersWhether regulators or auditors require explicit validity windows and automatic re-audit when hosted model aliases change.
3 · Memory control planesWhether major agent platforms expose standardized retention, deletion, provenance, poisoning and user-sovereignty controls.

One practical action for this week

Add a trajectory evidence row to the release checklist for every high-risk agent: protected data accessed, memory written or retrieved, tool calls proposed and admitted, outbound destinations, and model/tool versions. Do not approve a release based only on final-answer quality or aggregate task success.
