Today’s thesis: a control is not dependable merely because it blocks an attack, records an intent, or reports a fairness score. It must preserve the user’s actual scope, remain reproducible across execution environments, and reveal when its own evaluator is wrong.

Executive readout

Today’s strongest papers challenge three shortcuts: high prompt-injection attack success is not enough to train a robust defender; a logged intent contract cannot resolve an ambiguous object the executor cannot see; and an identical model can cross a fairness threshold when numerical precision changes. The practical move is to make scope, execution configuration, evaluator quality, and outcome evidence explicit release artifacts.

10 high-signal developments

Selected for reproducible evidence and direct implications for privacy engineering, agent security, evaluation, or governance. Publication status is explicit.

Privacy & security · 01

Federated averaging does not prevent exact text recovery

Omkar Shrikanth et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24689

Measured finding
An iterative gradient-inversion attack subtracts exactly recovered sentences from the aggregate to expose remaining contributions. The authors report 100% precision and exact recovery of up to 80.2% of client sentences when the aggregate contained up to four times more tokens than the model hidden dimension; prior attacks recovered none exactly in that regime.
Method & limitations
The result targets particular federated-learning text settings and aggregates; it does not establish recovery across all optimizers, clipping, secure aggregation, or differential-privacy configurations. It is an offensive preprint, not a field incident rate.
Why it matters
Aggregation alone should not be treated as a privacy boundary for language-model updates.
TPM application
Threat-model malicious clients, test reconstruction against realistic batch/round settings, and require secure aggregation plus quantified DP where sensitive text is trained.
Business outcome hypothesis
Reconstruction testing can prevent overstated privacy claims and reduce breach exposure, with possible utility and compute costs from stronger defenses.
Forward implication
Federated learning assurance will increasingly require empirical recovery tests, not architecture labels.
Privacy & security · 02

Lightweight verification narrows manipulation in local differential privacy

Haochen Sun, Jacob Imola & Xi He · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24623

Measured finding
A three-message proof-of-plaintext-evaluation framework restricts malicious LDP clients from arbitrary output manipulation to selective dropout. Mean per-client online latency stayed below 500 ms in evaluated PI-RAPPOR and Hadamard Response settings; the paper proves attack-dependent error improves by a factor proportional to the square root of domain size.
Method & limitations
The theoretical and experimental scope is histogram estimation with named mechanisms. Selective dropout remains possible, and latency/device/network behavior outside the test setup is not established.
Why it matters
Formal ε guarantees do not by themselves protect aggregate utility from malicious clients.
TPM application
Add poisoning/dropout abuse cases to the privacy review and select verification or server-side consistency checks based on whether clients can be changed.
Business outcome hypothesis
Lightweight validation may improve data quality without the cost of homomorphic encryption, but deployment overhead needs measurement.
Forward implication
Privacy telemetry platforms will need integrity controls alongside confidentiality guarantees.
Privacy & security · 03

SkillCFI compiles agent procedures into enforceable control flow

Bonan Ruan et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24185

Measured finding
Across 770 sessions, a deterministic enforcer blocked before dispatch all 240 tested skill violations that an allowlist admitted. Refusals listing legal next steps raised recovery from 21% to 65%; compact commands reduced reported slowdown from 1.32× to 1.08×.
Method & limitations
The compiler derives an executable contract from each skill and rejects obligations lacking trusted evidence. Tests cover eight self-written skills; none of 53 public skills compiled as written, which signals both rigor and substantial migration friction.
Why it matters
Tool permissions cannot ensure that required validation happens before a destructive operation.
TPM application
Compile high-risk skills into deterministic state machines, prohibit unmediated scripts, and require trusted evidence at each transition.
Business outcome hypothesis
Control-flow enforcement may reduce sequence-level incidents and improve auditability, while increasing skill-authoring and interface work.
Forward implication
Agent skill marketplaces may need enforceability diagnostics before installation or enterprise approval.
Privacy & security · 04

PIPETTE shows attack diversity matters more than red-team win rate

Lipeng He, Yihan Wang, Jiawen Zhang & N. Asokan · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24362

Measured finding
The authors find two RL red-teamers collapse onto similar prompt-injection strategies, so extra successful attacks add little adversarial-training value. PIPETTE uses dictionary-learning rewards for strategy coverage plus on-policy training; on AgentDojo and ASB it reportedly reaches near-zero attack success on static attacks and withstands adaptive attacks while preserving benign utility near the undefended baseline.
Method & limitations
The abstract does not provide one universal adaptive-ASR number. Results are benchmark- and agent-specific; robustness against novel attack families and real tool ecosystems remains unproven.
Why it matters
Optimizing attack success alone can produce redundant evidence and a falsely reassuring defense.
TPM application
Track attack-family coverage, marginal defense gain, benign utility, and holdout adaptive attacks as separate release metrics.
Business outcome hypothesis
Coverage-aware purple-teaming may improve defense yield per red-team dollar, but only if taxonomy gaps are monitored.
Forward implication
Security dashboards will shift from “how often did attacks win?” to “which behaviors have we actually covered and hardened?”
Privacy & security · 05

Intent contracts improve partial fulfillment but do not stop wrong-object actions

Ziyuan Wang, Genliang Zhu, Xiaobo Chen & Chu Wang · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24025

Measured finding
A tool-less planner submits a logged goal, verbatim user quote, and scope fields; a trusted model writes the call. On three AmPermBench families, contracts completed requested work in 27/30 over-reach runs versus 16/30 for an intent-visible reviewer, but did not reduce unauthorized actions. Wrong targets occurred in 40–49% of state-changing runs after environment terms were clarified.
Method & limitations
One planner/executor pair and benign over-reach—not adversarial planners—were tested. The trusted model could not see the environment needed to resolve ambiguous references.
Why it matters
A clean authorization interface cannot validate facts it is architecturally unable to observe.
TPM application
Bind contracts to resolved object identifiers and environment evidence; fail closed when reference resolution is ambiguous.
Business outcome hypothesis
Intent logging may improve traceability and partial task completion, but object-resolution evidence is necessary for risk reduction.
Forward implication
Agent authorization will move from text-only intent toward evidence-bound object capabilities.
Governance & reliability · 06

Reviewer calibration—not activity—predicts human oversight value

Priyanka Sinha & Sugam Pandey · 18 Nov 2026 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2611.24932

Measured finding
Using 541,621 reviewer events from 483 reviewers over six months in an AI-assisted accounting system, a behavior-only expertise profile ranked an overconfident cohort with 5.2% correction survival above genuine experts with 99.2%. Outcome-gated calibration concentrated 92.5% of human effort on genuine model errors.
Method & limitations
The production evidence is from one accounting system, and individual causal value is unidentifiable under majority-vote aggregation. Association and selection effects may not transfer to other review domains.
Why it matters
Review volume, speed, tenure, and agreement can reward confident but low-value oversight.
TPM application
Measure reviewer confidence against later outcomes and route cases to the better-calibrated human or model at the item level.
Business outcome hypothesis
Calibration-aware routing could reduce review load and improve defect catch rate, subject to outcome-label quality and fairness review.
Forward implication
Human-in-the-loop programs will be judged by calibrated intervention value, not seat count.
Governance & reliability · 07

Existing user-simulator metrics can reward acceptance of broken work

Hengguang Zhou et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24892

Measured finding
Metrics from three prior studies detected only 5–6 of ten deliberately injected simulator failures, and combined scores favored populations instructed to accept broken work. A proposed suite detected all ten. Evaluation covered six simulators and 351 real sessions from 348 users across 23 languages.
Method & limitations
Perturbations include goal changes, omitted constraints, early stopping, and accepting broken outputs. Detection on seeded failures does not prove realism for all user populations or production contexts.
Why it matters
A scalable synthetic-user evaluation can systematically overrate agents when the simulator stops enforcing the user’s actual goals.
TPM application
Meta-evaluate simulator metrics with seeded failures; report trace-level goal, constraint, and persona fidelity separately from population averages.
Business outcome hypothesis
Better simulator validation may reduce false launch confidence, while increasing benchmark construction cost.
Forward implication
Agent evaluations will need “simulator cards” explaining what failures their synthetic users can and cannot detect.
Governance & reliability · 08

Fairness verdicts can change with numerical execution contracts

Erzheng Zhang & Agnes Chu · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · under review · arXiv 2611.23657

Measured finding
Holding data, threshold, and audit code fixed, the study varied arithmetic and deployment precision across three tabular models and two LLMs. A 24B model served with vLLM FP8 selected 54% of people versus 45% under strict FP32 and passed a diagnostic sex audit the reference failed; paired uncertainty did not establish that FP8 was fairer.
Method & limitations
Tier 1 isolated arithmetic with byte-identical weights; Tier 2 also changed weights/kernels. Every changed pass/fail verdict had a sampling interval containing the criterion, so threshold proximity—not a robust fairness improvement—explains the instability.
Why it matters
Audit reproducibility depends on hardware, precision, kernels, group definitions, weights, and tests—not the model file alone.
TPM application
Version a numerical execution contract and report verdict ranges across approved deployment configurations separately from sampling uncertainty.
Business outcome hypothesis
Configuration-stability testing may prevent contradictory audit claims across environments and reduce remediation churn.
Forward implication
Assurance artifacts will increasingly bind fairness results to the serving stack, not only the trained model.
Governance & reliability · 09

Counterfactual audits expose visual agents’ fixed observation heuristics

Ruiqi Liu et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24867

Measured finding
Across forty paired routing anchors, five open VLMs made 0/40 optimal budget-dependent switches and lost 12.50–15.62 percentage points at anchors, although non-anchor average loss was only 0.59–0.85 points. In three-read tasks, first-read loss was 0.18–0.96 points while later reads lost 22.20–26.76 points.
Method & limitations
The protocol fixes evidence and bindings while changing budget/value, then computes exact continuation loss. Controlled routing tasks and 30 FUNSD forms may not represent all visual workflows; hosted-model protocols differ.
Why it matters
End accuracy can hide a fixed “always inspect this region” heuristic that fails when time or token budget changes.
TPM application
Pair evaluations at different observation budgets and attribute loss by step before claiming adaptive planning.
Business outcome hypothesis
Budget-paired testing may avoid expensive visual pipelines that consume more tokens without improving decisions.
Forward implication
Agent benchmarks will increasingly audit the value of each observation, not just the final answer.
Governance & reliability · 10

Self-improving agents store learning in executable code

Zipeng Qiu et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24590

Measured finding
Across 170 GameWorld tasks with at most eight one-minute executions per run, a model that wrote, tested, and revised a program between attempts reached 47.80% mean progress, versus 30.90% for a trained 2B real-time controller, and led across all five game categories.
Method & limitations
The comparison is in visual games, with bounded attempts and study-specific controllers. Progress is not task completion, and executable self-modification introduces security and regression risks not quantified by the headline result.
Why it matters
Executable artifacts can preserve learning more effectively than online reasoning or rapid weight updates.
TPM application
Sandbox self-authored code, diff every revision, run invariant/security/retention tests, and promote only signed versions through change control.
Business outcome hypothesis
Offline code synthesis may improve latency and learning reuse, but validation cost and attack surface could offset gains.
Forward implication
Self-improving agents will resemble software supply chains, requiring provenance, tests, approvals, and rollback.

Top impactful research and applications

Five studies with the clearest path from evidence to an operational control. Proposed applications and impacts are hypotheses unless the study evaluated them directly.

SkillCFI Preprint · 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: deterministic enforcement, formal enforceability criterion, mutation testing, 770 sessions, and transparent migration failures. Limit: eight authored skills and zero of 53 public skills compiling unchanged constrain generalization.

Plausible application: compile high-risk privacy remediation or data-deletion skills into evidence-bearing state machines. Expected impact hypothesis: fewer sequence violations and stronger audit trails, at the cost of redesigning interfaces.

Intent Contracts Preprint · 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: useful negative result distinguishes logged intent from environment-grounded authorization. Limit: one model pair and benign over-reach.

Plausible application: add resolved object IDs, scopes, and evidence hashes to agent approvals. Expected impact hypothesis: better traceability and partial fulfillment; risk reduction only when ambiguity is externally resolved.

User-simulator meta-evaluation Preprint · 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: ten seeded failure modes plus 351 real multilingual sessions reveal blind spots in prior metrics. Limit: success at seeded-failure detection is not full ecological validity.

Plausible application: simulator fault injection before an enterprise-agent benchmark is used for release decisions. Expected impact hypothesis: fewer false positives in agent readiness decisions.

Numerical execution contracts for fairness Preprint · 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: separates byte-identical arithmetic effects from reduced-precision configuration effects across multiple hardware stacks. Limit: no stable evidence that a changed configuration is fairer.

Plausible application: attach precision, engine, kernel, threshold, group, weighting, and uncertainty metadata to every fairness result. Expected impact hypothesis: more reproducible audits and fewer contradictory claims.

Exact text recovery from federated learning Preprint · 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: exact-match precision and recovery rates directly test confidentiality failure. Limit: attack transfer across realistic defenses and production FL configurations is not established.

Plausible application: make reconstruction resistance a release test for federated language training. Expected impact hypothesis: more honest privacy claims and earlier identification of necessary DP/security controls.

Research to Build: Evidence-Bound Tool Authorization

Goal: prototype a control that preserves user intent, resolves target objects against trusted state, enforces legal action order, and produces replayable evidence. This combines the useful structure of intent contracts with SkillCFI’s deterministic enforcement—and directly tests the wrong-object failure contracts did not solve.

Prototype

  1. Choose one high-risk workflow: delete data, change access, or send regulated information.
  2. Compile the skill into legal states and transitions; require trusted evidence for every precondition.
  3. Translate the user request into a contract containing the verbatim request, action, resolved immutable object ID, scope, and evidence hash.
  4. Let the planner propose but never dispatch; a deterministic enforcer admits only contract-valid calls.

Evaluation & gate

  • Seed 100 paired cases: clear targets, ambiguous references, over-reach, reordered steps, injected instructions, and stale objects.
  • Primary metric: unauthorized or wrong-object dispatch rate; target zero on seeded critical cases.
  • Secondary: requested-task completion, false blocks, recovery after refusal, latency, and evidence completeness.
  • Promotion: no critical miss across three independent model seeds; every admitted call replayable from logged evidence; automatic rollback on invariant failure.

Evidence boundary: this is a proposed experiment, not a proven enterprise control. Shadow-test it against real workflow distributions and have privacy/security reviewers adjudicate a blinded sample before production use.

Three near-term signals to watch

Changes that would materially alter today’s control recommendations.

1 · Adaptive robustness

Whether PIPETTE’s defense gains reproduce against independent, taxonomy-external attackers and live tool ecosystems—not only benchmark attack families.

2 · Execution-bound audits

Whether standards or regulators begin requiring precision, inference engine, kernels, thresholds, and uncertainty ranges in AI audit evidence.

3 · Agent artifact supply chains

Whether self-authored code, skills, and memories gain standardized provenance, signing, sandbox, retention, and rollback controls across major platforms.

One practical action for this week

Add two fields to every high-risk agent tool approval: “resolved object evidence” and “execution contract.” The first must identify the exact target from trusted state; the second must define permitted action order and preconditions. Reject approvals that rely only on the planner’s natural-language goal or a broad tool allowlist.
