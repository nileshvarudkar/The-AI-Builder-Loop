Privacy & security · 01

Sparse topology can defeat decentralized secure aggregation

Wenrui Yu, Changlong Ji, Johannes Bjerva & Qiongxiu Li · 8 Sep 2026; ACM CCS ’26 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2609.08476 · DOI

Measured finding
Across image, tabular and text tasks under sparse decentralized topologies, colluding semi-honest nodes reconstructed honest participants’ local updates, enabling downstream recovery of private training data.
Method & limitations
The attack maps asymmetric neighborhood aggregates to a Hidden Subset Sum problem, then combines lattice reduction with structural filtering. It assumes sparse topology, hidden aggregation coefficients and colluding semi-honest nodes; the result does not invalidate every secure-aggregation deployment.
Why it matters
Privacy depends on observation geometry, not just the aggregation primitive.
TPM application
Add topology, collusion and repeated-view analysis to federated-learning privacy reviews.
Business outcome hypothesis
Topology-aware threat modeling may prevent false assurances and late protocol redesign.
Forward implication
Secure-aggregation claims will increasingly state graph and collusion assumptions explicitly.
Privacy & security · 02

Compromised training components can store private examples in model weights

Jin-Seong Kim et al. · 30 Jul 2026; ACM CCS ’26 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2607.27886

Measured finding
GradLock reported near-lossless reconstruction (SSIM about 1.0) and extraction under one second across MNIST, Imagenette and CelebA. In a deployment study, 93.3% of participants failed to detect the malicious logic.
Method & limitations
Training-time injection uses deterministic indexing and dynamic gradient locking to preserve an isolated parameter payload. Selected vision datasets and attack-controlled components do not establish real-world prevalence.
Why it matters
Post-training model review can miss a malicious dependency that has already embedded data.
TPM application
Require signed trainers, reproducible builds and synthetic-canary extraction tests.
Business outcome hypothesis
Behavioral supply-chain tests may reduce the chance of shipping a privacy-compromised artifact.
Forward implication
AI software bills of materials will need execution evidence, not inventory alone.
Privacy & security · 03

Exact client text can be recovered from federated aggregates

Omkar Shrikanth et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24689

Measured finding
An iterative inversion attack reported 100% precision and exact recovery of up to 80.2% of sentences when aggregates held up to four times more tokens than the hidden dimension; prior attacks recovered none exactly in that regime.
Method & limitations
Recovered sentences are subtracted from the aggregate before the next recovery round. Configuration-specific results do not establish success against secure aggregation, clipping or calibrated DP.
Why it matters
Keeping raw text local does not make shared updates non-sensitive.
TPM application
Run reconstruction tests and demand quantified defenses for federated language training.
Business outcome hypothesis
Attack-based validation can prevent overstated privacy claims.
Forward implication
Federated reviews will require empirical recoverability evidence.
Privacy & security · 04

Federated-learning controls can produce cryptographically auditable dataflow

Jinnan Guo, Kapil Vaswani, Andrew Paverd & Peter Pietzuch · revised 14 Nov 2026; ACM SoCC ’26 · Peer-reviewed conference paper · arXiv 2412.10537 · DOI

Measured finding
ExclaveFL creates runtime-attested dataflow graphs for client transformations and server aggregation. An AMD SEV-SNP-compatible variant added less than 12% overhead versus unprotected training.
Method & limitations
Integrity-only execution environments generate proofs without software-managed secrets. Native exclaves need proprietary firmware; the compatible variant has weaker guarantees and cannot prove source-data truthfulness.
Why it matters
Auditors can verify that required sanitization or aggregation executed.
TPM application
Pilot machine-verifiable evidence for critical training transformations.
Business outcome hypothesis
Attestation may lower audit friction across organizational boundaries.
Forward implication
Model lineage will link to executable evidence graphs.
Privacy & security · 05

Local differential privacy does not guarantee honest telemetry

Haochen Sun, Jacob Imola & Xi He · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24623

Measured finding
A three-message proof-of-plaintext-evaluation design restricted manipulation to selective dropout with mean per-client online latency below 500 ms in evaluated PI-RAPPOR and Hadamard Response settings. Theory gives a square-root-of-domain-size reduction in attack-dependent error versus unrestricted manipulation.
Method & limitations
The study covers histogram estimation and selected mechanisms; dropout remains possible and production device/network costs are unknown.
Why it matters
Private reports can still be malicious reports.
TPM application
Pair privacy-budget reviews with poisoning, replay, report-validity and dropout tests.
Business outcome hypothesis
Lightweight verification may improve aggregate quality without full homomorphic-computation cost.
Forward implication
Telemetry assurance will combine confidentiality and integrity.
Governance & reliability · 06

Multi-model enterprise adoption creates one missing control-plane problem

Fabricio F. Costa · 31 Jul 2026 · Preprint / evidence synthesis · arXiv 2609.31622

Reported finding
The synthesis reports 81% of Global 2000 enterprises running three or more model families and 79% of enterprises exceeding AI budgets; FinOps-mature organizations reportedly overshot by a mean 30.9%. It argues governance, cost and telemetry fragmentation share one absent cross-provider abstraction.
Method & limitations
The paper combines surveys, transactions, provider disclosures, case studies and pricing documentation. These are heterogeneous secondary sources, not a controlled enterprise experiment; reported percentages inherit source limitations.
Why it matters
Vendor-by-vendor controls do not produce portfolio-level risk, cost or usage evidence.
TPM application
Normalize model identity, policy decisions, cost-per-pass and telemetry across gateways and providers.
Business outcome hypothesis
A common control plane may reduce shadow AI, duplicated integrations and cost surprises.
Forward implication
AI governance platforms will resemble cloud-management planes more than static registries.
Governance & reliability · 07

Agent risk tiers need critical-dimension overrides

Hannah M. Liu, Rhea Saxena & Shiv Asthana · 10 Jul 2026 · Preprint / governance proposal · arXiv 2607.09586

Proposed claim
TrustX ARC combines seven agent types, a 12-dimension scoring rubric, autonomy classification and three governance tiers. Its critical-dimension rule prevents a severe risk from being averaged into a moderate composite score.
Method & limitations
The framework is grounded in prior governance frameworks and demonstrated through an illustrative example. It is not empirically validated against incident prediction, inter-rater reliability or operational outcomes.
Why it matters
An agent with broad write authority or sensitive-data access should not pass because other dimensions score low.
TPM application
Add non-averagable triggers for irreversible actions, privileged access, regulated data and autonomous delegation.
Business outcome hypothesis
Hard stops may reduce under-classification, at the cost of more escalations.
Forward implication
Agent intake will distinguish risk scoring from risk vetoes.
Governance & reliability · 08

Formal agent contracts can conserve resources through delegation

Qing Ye & Jing Tan · revised 25 Mar 2026; COINE 2026 workshop · Accepted workshop paper · arXiv 2601.08815

Measured finding
Across four experiments, Agent Contracts reported 90% token reduction with 525× lower variance in iterative workflows, zero conservation violations during multi-agent delegation and measurable quality–resource tradeoffs.
Method & limitations
A formal contract covers inputs, outputs, multidimensional resources, time, success criteria and delegation conservation laws. Four experimental settings do not establish general production reliability or adversarial enforcement.
Why it matters
Subagents can multiply spend and runtime unless child budgets remain bounded by parent authority.
TPM application
Encode token, time, tool, data and approval budgets in machine-enforced execution contracts.
Business outcome hypothesis
Budget conservation may improve predictability and auditability without eliminating quality tradeoffs.
Forward implication
Agent orchestration will adopt policy-as-code contracts and hierarchical budget proofs.
Governance & reliability · 09

User-simulator metrics can reward acceptance of broken work

Hengguang Zhou et al. · 18 Nov 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2611.24892

Measured finding
Methods from three prior studies detected only 5–6 of ten injected simulator failures; combined scores favored populations told to accept broken work. A new suite detected all ten.
Method & limitations
Six simulators were compared with 351 real sessions from 348 users across 23 languages. Seeded faults do not prove realism for every domain.
Why it matters
A synthetic benchmark can make an agent look better by weakening the simulated user.
TPM application
Fault-inject changed goals, omitted constraints, early stops and broken-work acceptance before adopting simulator gates.
Business outcome hypothesis
Meta-evaluation may reduce false launch confidence.
Forward implication
Evaluators will publish failure-detection coverage like test suites publish coverage.
Governance & reliability · 10

Self-evolving agents improve aggregate scores while forgetting

Xiaoyin Chen et al. · 19 Oct 2026 · Preprint · arXiv 2610.25858

Measured finding
Across four drifting streams built from BIRD, BFCL and ScienceWorld, ReasoningBank, GEPA and Meta-Harness improved aggregate performance on most streams, but greater plasticity consistently reduced stability; retrieval showed foreign-memory and workflow-collision failures.
Method & limitations
Three frozen-weight approaches cover experience retrieval and harness self-modification. Results do not define a universal frontier for proprietary or production agents.
Why it matters
Average improvement can mask regression on old and unseen work.
TPM application
Gate every memory, prompt or skill mutation on retention, current-task gain and unseen-task transfer.
Business outcome hypothesis
Regression-aware change control may reduce silent workflow damage.
Forward implication
Self-evolution will be governed like software release—with diffs, canaries and rollback.

Top impactful research and applications

Topology-based secure-aggregation attack — ACM CCS ’26

Evidence quality: formal reduction plus experiments across three data modalities. Limit: sparse topology and collusion assumptions. Application hypothesis: graph-based privacy threat modeling can identify deployments where local aggregate views expose solvable linear structure. Expected impact: fewer architecture-level privacy blind spots.

Agent Contracts — accepted COINE 2026 workshop paper

Evidence quality: formal lifecycle and conservation laws with four experiments. Limit: limited workload scope and no adversarial deployment study. Application hypothesis: machine-enforced parent/child budgets can bound token, time and tool use. Expected impact: more predictable cost and delegation evidence.

TrustX ARC — preprint / governance proposal

Evidence quality: structured synthesis and an operational rubric. Limit: illustrative rather than validated. Application hypothesis: critical-dimension overrides can prevent broad authority from disappearing inside an average score. Expected impact: more conservative agent tiering; operational effectiveness remains untested.

User-simulator meta-evaluation — preprint, 18 Nov 2026

Evidence quality: ten injected failures plus 351 real multilingual sessions. Limit: seeded-fault coverage is incomplete realism. Application hypothesis: evaluator fault-detection should be a prerequisite for synthetic-user release evidence. Expected impact: fewer misleading benchmark passes.

Self-evolving continual agents — preprint, 19 Oct 2026

Evidence quality: three methods across four drifting streams. Limit: benchmark and method scope. Application hypothesis: mutation-level retention, collision and transfer gates can make self-evolution a controlled release process. Expected impact: lower regression risk, not guaranteed performance gain.

Research to Build: Cross-Provider Agent Contract Gate

Goal: test a vendor-neutral release contract that combines critical-risk vetoes, delegated resource conservation and evaluator fault injection.

Prototype

  1. Select 30 representative workflows across two model providers and one self-hosted model.
  2. Define a contract for identity, permitted data, tool scopes, token/time/cost budgets, success criteria and human-approval points.
  3. Set hard-stop triggers for regulated data, irreversible external actions, privilege escalation and unconstrained subagent creation.
  4. Inject budget delegation, wrong-recipient, model-switch, broken-work-acceptance and stale-memory failures.

Metrics and gate

  • Task success and cost per successful task—not cost per attempt.
  • Critical-risk false-negative rate and escalation volume.
  • Parent/child budget conservation, tool-policy violations and provider telemetry completeness.
  • Release gate: zero critical misses; zero budget-conservation violations; reproducible cross-provider trace; no regression on retained workflows.

Evidence boundary This combined control is a proposed experiment. ARC, Agent Contracts and the simulator study motivate its components; none validates the integrated gate in an enterprise environment.

Three near-term signals to watch

1 · Topology-aware privacy claimsWhether decentralized FL frameworks expose collusion and observation-graph assumptions in deployment evidence.
2 · Non-averagable agent risksWhether enterprise intake systems add veto dimensions for privilege, sensitive data, delegation and irreversible action.
3 · Cross-provider cost-of-passWhether gateways normalize model version, success, retries and total agent spend across providers.

One practical action

Add a “critical dimension” column to your next AI agent intake. For each risk, record whether it can be averaged, requires mandatory escalation or blocks release. Start with regulated data access, external write authority, autonomous delegation and irreversible actions.
